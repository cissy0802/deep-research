## 票 1:CORRECTED(单源已核)

**修正后表述**:Epoch's 'The plunging price of thought' (2026-09-22) finds that, across five math, science and game benchmarks, the cost of reaching a given performance level has fallen about 47% per quarter since 2023 (about 13x per year). The decline is about 66% per quarter right after a level becomes SOTA and about 32% per quarter two years later. Among the eight traces in the figure, only one has an open-weights model as the 'second dot'. Cottier et al. find that the price at fixed benchmark performance falls 5–10x per year, while the cost of running frontier models on GPQA-D, SWE-bench V and AIME rises 3–18x per year. Snell et al. (2024) find that under FLOPs-matched comparison, on problems where the small model already has a non-trivial success rate, test-time compute can beat a model 14x larger. Toby Ord estimates from OpenAI's charts that 'a 10x scaling of RL is required to get the same performance boost as a 3x scaling of inference'.

**口径修正**:
- (a) All three figures and the quote are verbatim and HOLD. Add the scope: five benchmarks (math, sciences, games), and the faster decline at SOTA appears in only 3 of 5 benchmarks. 66% per quarter means 75x per year.
- (b) Verbatim HOLDS. The 3–18x per year is the cost of running the frontier model on three benchmarks (GPQA-D, SWE-bench V, AIME), not a per-token price.
- (c) Verbatim HOLDS. Keep the qualifiers 'FLOPs-matched' and 'on problems where the smaller model already has a non-trivial success rate'. The setup is PaLM 2-S* on MATH. The paper also finds that on the hardest problems pretraining compute is more effective.
- (d) Verbatim HOLDS. Label it as Ord's rough estimate from reading OpenAI's charts, not a controlled experiment.

**口径修正**:

- (b) Wrong attribution: 'Cottier et al. The Price of Progress' → Gundlach, Lynch, Mertens, Thompson (MIT FutureTech), arXiv 2511.23455. The figures and quotes are correct. Add: '3–18x' is the rising price of running frontier models on benchmarks, driven by larger models and longer reasoning

- (d) HOLDS. Label it Ord's heuristic estimate from OpenAI's public charts

**核对过程**:(a) The Epoch report (datePublished 2026-09-22, Emberson & Roodman) reads word for word: 'fallen about 47% per quarter since 2023, or 13× per year'; 'cost falls 66% per quarter (75× per year) for performance that has just debuted as SOTA. Two years later, prices fall half as fast, at 32% per quarter (4.7× per year)'. The report's own Discussion section has: 'In only one of the eight traces in the figure does the model responsible for the second dot have open weights (QwQ-32B for 25% performance on AIME)'. Recomputation: 0.53^4 → 12.7x per year, which checks out. (b) arXiv 2511.23455 'The Price of Progress' is authored by Hans Gundlach, Jayson Lynch, Matthias Mertens and Neil Thompson (MIT FutureTech), v2 dated 2026-03-23. It is not by Cottier et al., who are only cited inside it for '10–1,000×'. The quotes match verbatim: 'decreased remarkably fast, around 5× to 10× per year, for frontier models on knowledge, reasoning, math, and software engineering benchmarks' and 'the price of running frontier models is rising between 3× to 18× per year due to bigger models and larger reasoning demands'. He infers this from public OpenAI o1→o3→GPT-5 charts, so it is a heuristic, not an experiment.
