## 票 1:CORRECTED(单源已核)

**修正后表述**:METR's open-source evidence is entirely on TH1.0 and stops at 2025-11. A self-fit of METR's public TH1.0 runs gives open-model lags (when the closed-model envelope first reached the same p50, with log interpolation) of roughly 6.2 months for DeepSeek-V3, 3.4 for R1, 6.8 for R1-0528, 7.2 for gpt-oss-120b, and 8 for Kimi K2 Thinking. A step-function calculation gives 3–7 months, so the lag shows a mild widening at most and depends on the method. Against the best closed model available at each release, the p50 ratio went from about 1.5x for R1 to about 2.8x for R1-0528, then settled around 2.3–2.5x. Kimi K2 Thinking measured about 54 minutes (CI 25–100) through a third-party inference provider, and METR flagged lower confidence in that estimate. The figure of about 3.7x mixes TH1.0 and TH1.1 and should not be used. METR itself wrote in 2025-02 that open-weight models were 'on par with state-of-the-art models from 6 months ago', and in 2025-06 that mid-2025 DeepSeek models matched late-2024 frontier models. METR has never measured an open-source model on TH1.1, so its open-source lag data ends with Kimi K2 Thinking in 2025-11.

**口径修正**:

## 票 2:CORRECTED(方向存争)

**修正后表述**:Refitting METR's TH1.0 raw runs (weighted logistic, interpolated on the running-max envelope of closed models) gives open-model lags behind the closed frontier of about 6.2 months for DeepSeek-V3, 3.4 for R1, 6.8 for R1-0528, 7.2 for gpt-oss-120b and 8.2 for Kimi K2 Thinking (2025-11). With a step rule instead of interpolation, the figures are 3.0–6.7 months. At release, the best closed model's 50% horizon was 1.5x R1's, 2.8x R1-0528's and about 2.3–2.5x Kimi K2 Thinking's (GPT-5 about 131–137 vs Kimi about 54–58 minutes). Any '3.7x' figure mixes the TH1.0 and TH1.1 versions and is invalid. METR's own reports at the time put DeepSeek-V3 'on par with state-of-the-art models from 6 months ago' and R1 at 7 months behind. They also said mid-2025 DeepSeek models were similar to 'frontier models from late 2024'. METR has never measured an open-weight model on TH1.1; the last one was Kimi K2 Thinking, added on 2025-11-19. On a different scale, the independent CAISI evaluation in 2026-04/05 found DeepSeek V4 Pro about 8 months behind the US frontier.

**口径修正**:
- (a) Lags 6.2/3.4/6.8/7.2/8.2 months hold only under the interpolated running-max envelope. Under the step rule they are 3.4/3.0/5.7/5.3/6.7. The method must be stated, or both given as a range.
- (a) Release-time ratios 1.5x → 2.8x → 2.3–2.4x hold on the refit. With METR's published TH1.0 values, Kimi is 137.3/54.2 = 2.5x, so write about 2.3–2.5x.
- (b) Ruling: 判死: 'Kimi K2 Thinking 54 vs GPT-5 high 203 → 0.27 (about 3.7x)' mixes TH1.0 and TH1.1. Use about 2.3–2.5x within TH1.0, with the closed baseline taken as the best closed model released on or before the open model's date. The catch-up time of about 8.2–8.4 months can stay.
- (b) 'GPT-5 TH1.0 约 131 分钟' is my refit value. METR/Epoch publish 137.3 minutes (medium), so cite the source when writing.
- (c) Both quotes are verbatim. Add the contrary evidence: METR's R1 report said 'on-par with state-of-the-art models from 7 months ago', far from the self-computed R1 lag of 3.4 months. METR's contemporaneous verdicts and the retrospective refit use different measures and should not be strung together as one series.
- (d) Holds. You may add CAISI's 2026-05 measurement of DeepSeek V4 Pro as an independent check on whether the 'lag of about 8 months' still held in 2026. Its scale is different and should be labelled.

**核对过程**:Recomputation method: for each model I fit a logistic of score_binarized on log2(human_minutes), weighted by invsqrt_task_weight. p50 = 2^(-b0/b1). L2 strength C = 1e9, 10 and 1 gives identical results. The closed models' running-max p50 forms the envelope, and lag = open model's release date minus the date the envelope reaches its p50, interpolated in log space. Result: V3 6.2, R1 3.4, V3-0324 6.1, R1-0528 6.8, gpt-oss-120b 7.2, Kimi K2 Thinking 8.2 months (Kimi p50 57.6, GPT-5 130.8). This reproduces the claimed numbers exactly. Method sensitivity: using the step rule (date of the first closed model with p50 at or above the open model's) instead gives V3 3.4, R1 3.0, R1-0528 5.7, gpt-oss 5.3, Kimi 6.7 months. Lags are therefore method-dependent by about 1.5–3 months, and the method must be stated. Ratios at release (best closed model released on or before the open model's date): R1 1.49x (o1), R1-0528 2.79x (o3), gpt-oss 2.42x (Opus 4.1), Kimi 2.27x (GPT-5). These reproduce. Ruling on (b): Epoch's METR mirror has a blank-version (TH1.0-family) row with kimi-k2-thinking 54.2 and gpt-5_medium 137.3, while GPT-5 high at 203.0 is a METR-Horizon-v1.1 value. The 0.27 (3.7x) therefore divides a TH1.0 Kimi figure by a TH1.1 GPT-5 figure and is invalid. Comparing within TH1.0 gives 2.27x on my refit and 137.3/54.2 = 2.53x on METR's published values. The correct range is about 2.3–2.5x. A catch-up time of about 8.2–8.4 months is consistent across both lines. Note that my GPT-5 TH1.0 refit is 130.8 while METR/Epoch publish 137.3 (medium), so '约 131' comes from the refit, not METR's official number. (c) Quotes check: the V3 report (2025-02-12) says 'the performance of open-weight models on our tasks is on par with state-of-the-art models from 6 months ago'. The 2025-06-27 report says 'the level of autonomous capabilities of mid-2025 DeepSeek models is similar to the level of capabilities of frontier models from late 2024'. Contrary evidence: the R1 report (2025-03-05) says 'on-par with state–of-the-art models from 7 months ago'. My recomputed R1 lag is 3.4 months, and the step method gives 3.0, so METR's own contemporaneous verdict disagrees with the self-computed figure by a factor of about 2. METR's early reports were based on general-autonomy tasks and RE-Bench, not today's TH1.0 refit, which is why they differ. (d) TH1.1 yaml contains only closed models. The last open-model entry in the update log is 'November 19th, 2025: Added ... Kimi K2 Thinking'. The Frontier Risk Report evaluates no open-weight model and mentions DeepSeek only via CAISI's PortBench. This holds. Independent corroboration of the size of the lag: CAISI (NIST, 2026-05-01) says 'DeepSeek V4's capabilities lag behind the frontier by about 8 months' and 'performs similarly to GPT-5, which was released about 8 months ago'.
