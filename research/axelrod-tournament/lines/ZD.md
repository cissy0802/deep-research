# ZD line — zero-determinant strategies (summary)

28 claims in ZD.json. Most are verbatim-checked via PMC / arXiv / Edge.

## Press & Dyson 2012 (PNAS 109:10409, online 2012-05-21)
- What ZD is: memory-one strategies. X can unilaterally enforce a linear relation between the two long-run payoffs, or fix Y's score, but she cannot fix her own (Z01).
- Extortion (factor χ, example χ=3): an "evolutionary" opponent's best response is to accede. "Only a player with a theory of mind about his opponent can do better", and then the IPD becomes an ultimatum game (Z02, Z04).
- "Evolutionary" is defined as a single opponent who adapts his own strategy without modelling X. It does not mean population evolution. "Dominate" appears only in the title (Z03).
- TFT is named as a special, fair ZD case (χ=1 boundary). The exact parameter value sits in equation images and is unverified (Z05).
- If both players know ZD, the paper suggests they negotiate to full cooperation (Z06).

## Evolutionary fate
- **Stewart & Plotkin 2012 commentary.** In an Axelrod-like tournament, ZDGTFT-2 had the top total score, above TFT and GTFT. Extort-2 won the most head-to-head matches (after ALLD) but scored lower. Tournament parameters are not given (Z12, Z13).
- **Stewart & Plotkin 2013 PNAS.** Extortion fails unless N is very small. Generous ZD strategies are robust for all but the smallest N, and every robust strategy is generous. WSLS is not robust. Generous ZD beats WSLS only "in some regimes" (Z14, Z15).
- **Adami & Hintze 2013 Nat Commun 4:2193.**
  - ZD is at most weakly dominant and not an ESS.
  - ZD wins every match against Pavlov, yet Pavlov is the ESS and drives ZD extinct.
  - Only a tag-based ZD that recognises its own kind can be stable, and that advantage is short-lived.
  - Setup: N=1024, payoffs (3,0,5,1).
  - Correction to the brief: the stabilising "recognition" is ZD recognising ZD, not opponents recognising ZD (Z16–Z18).
- **Hilbe, Nowak & Sigmund 2013 PNAS.**
  - Extortion acts as a catalyst for cooperation, not as a stable outcome.
  - It holds its ground only in very small populations (M≤10 with b=3, c=1).
  - Red King effect in two-population coevolution (Z19).

## Human experiments
- **Hilbe, Röhl & Milinski 2014 Nat Commun 5:3976.** The correct title is "...but is **finally punished**...", not "outperformed in groups", and there was no group treatment.
  - Design: 60 subjects, 60 rounds, subjects not told they faced a computer.
  - Extortioners beat every human but earned less than generous ZD.
  - Humans cooperated 34.2% with extortioners vs 67.7% with generous ZD; withholding cooperation acted as punishment (Z20, Z21).
- **Wang et al. 2016 Nat Commun 7:11125.**
  - Extortion beat generosity only with 500 rounds AND subjects aware the opponent was a computer (2.943 vs 2.263 per round).
  - With 60 rounds there was no difference; when subjects were unaware, generosity won.
  - Author order is disputed: PMC lists Wang first; HCN 2018 lists Xu first (Z22).

## Reviews and the "wins every match, loses tournaments" framing
- **Hilbe, Chatterjee & Nowak 2018 NHB.** Extortioners never lose a direct competition and act as catalysts, but evolution "leads from extortion to generosity". The review sorts strategies into partners and rivals:
  - small N and few rounds favour rivals;
  - large N and stable relationships favour partners;
  - TFT is both partner and rival only in infinite, undiscounted games (Z23–Z25).
- **Hilbe, Traulsen & Sigmund 2015.** Published in *Games and Economic Behavior*, not the journal *Games* (Z26).
- **Knight et al. 2024 PLoS ONE.** Tested 204 strategies: extortioners get "a high number of wins" but not a high score. Top scorers extort only weak opponents (Z27).
- **Who says "wins pairwise but loses":** Stewart & Plotkin 2012 (Fig 1), Adami & Hintze 2013 ("win every single matchup"), Knight et al. 2024.
- **Mirror image of Axelrod:** HCN 2018 notes TFT "did not win any pairwise encounter" yet topped the tournament.
