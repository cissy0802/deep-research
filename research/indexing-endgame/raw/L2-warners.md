### CROSS-CALIBER ISSUES

**1. "Passive share" has at least five incompatible denominators, and the warning side moves between them.**
- Bogle 2018: index *mutual funds* as % of **total US stock-market value** = 17% (2018).
- Goldman Sachs (via Institutional Investor, Jan 2025): passive ownership of the **typical S&P 500 company** = 26%, up from 18% twenty years earlier.
- Green (Jan 2025): "about 45 percent" — explicitly **including index funds, futures, total return swaps, and option hedging based on indices**.
- Green (Jan 2026): "about 54% passive **by market share**" — denominator never defined; +4pts in 2025.
- Terry Smith (Dec 2025): "more than 50% of **assets**"; and separately "less than 10% of **funds**" in 2000 — denominator switches inside one sentence.
- Vanguard (Aug 2025): index funds as % of **MSCI ACWI market cap**, and separately as % of **US exchange trading volume** (1.2%).
The article's "passive has crossed 50%" premise is only true under some of these. **Green's 54% and Goldman's 26% are both defensible and differ by 2x purely on definition.**

**2. Ownership share vs trading share is the central equivocation, and it is where the entire debate actually lives.**
Vanguard: 1.2% of trading. Green: 20% direct + 8% derivatives + 36% market-making = 64%, vs 10% for active managers (down from 80% in 1995). Both were published in the same Institutional Investor article and neither side reconciles the buckets. Green's 36% "passive market making" is the single largest and least-defined term in the debate; L1/L3 should try to find its derivation. Vanguard's "cash-flow adjusted turnover" excludes ETF secondary trading, which is arguably the largest passive-adjacent volume of all.

**3. The $1 → $5 multiplier is systematically mis-attributed.**
Gabaix & Koijen's 5–8x is an estimate for **aggregate flows into the equity market**, over a **time-period average**, and is not a measurement of index-fund flow impact. Green explicitly says so in the transcript, then his claim that "it's rising with passive share" is sourced to "my own research" (unpublished) plus Haddad/Huebner/Loualiche. Asness reads the *same* Haddad et al. paper and concludes the magnitude cannot explain much. Media repetition has collapsed all of this into "every $1 into index funds adds $5 of market cap," which no cited source states.

**4. Green's own model over-predicts and he says so.**
His arithmetic yields ~+23%/yr for Apple from passive flow alone, which he immediately notes "is actually more than Apple gained last year." The rescue (Apple's fundamentals deteriorated, offsetting the flow push) makes the model unfalsifiable: any shortfall is fundamentals, any excess is flows. This is the cleanest available demonstration that the flows model, as publicly specified, has no rejection region.

**5. Bogle is cited by the warning side for a claim he did not make and explicitly rejected.**
The 2018 WSJ op-ed is about **voting control and corporate governance**, contains no bubble/price-discovery claim, and closes by defending index funds and endorsing Coates's "a ban on indexing would clearly not be a good idea." The 2017 "chaos, catastrophe" line is always quoted without the immediately following "what are the chances of everybody indexing? It's zero," "so, I'm not concerned about it," and his ">75% and not dangerous." Asness's footnote supplies the killer provenance: pressed on where 75% came from, Bogle said **"I made it up."** Any article using Bogle as a witness for the prosecution should be corrected on this point.

**6. Burry's famous quote and Burry's actual prediction are different claims with different track records.**
The CDO analogy (price discovery) is unfalsifiable and untested. The *liquidity* claim (small-caps and ETF exits) is specific, dated, and testable — and has never been meaningfully checked against Mar-2020 / Aug-2024 / Apr-2025 by anyone I found. Meanwhile the index he warned about is +165.5% since the interview. And the man himself no longer argues the case: since Nov 2025 he has deregistered his fund and pivoted to an AI-capex-accounting thesis.

**7. Conflicts of interest run in both directions and neither side is clean.**
- **Pro-bubble:** Einhorn (active value, explains 2010s underperformance); Terry Smith (down 14.1pp vs comparator in H1 2026, changed his process); Singer (activist fund selling the exact governance service he says indexers don't provide); Burry (small-cap value, now selling a $379/yr newsletter); **Green (as of 2026 launched Tier1 Alpha, an ETF/SMA shop whose entire alpha source is passive flow — the most direct monetization of the thesis in the field).**
- **Anti-bubble:** Vanguard (largest indexer; the Aug-2025 paper is a defense document); S&P DJI/SPIVA (index provider); BlackRock.

These three are the most useful adversarial anchors.

**8. Green's Jan-2026 "five years out the world comes to an end" is the only dated, falsifiable forecast in the corpus — and he disclaims it in the same paragraph.**
83% is stated as an **outer limit** derived under assumptions he lists as unrealistic (zero flows, all non-passive money fully active at elasticity 0.75–0.8, no circuit breakers, no policy response); he says realistic assumptions move the threshold "significantly lower." So the ~2031 date is simultaneously the most quotable and the most heavily caveated number on the warning side. Note also it rests on the undefined "54% and +4%/yr" series.
