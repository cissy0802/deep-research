### CROSS-CALIBER ISSUES

**1. "Passive crossed 50%" has at least five defensible dates.** 2019 (US equity funds only, Morningstar); 2023 (all US long-term funds, Morningstar, $13.29T vs $13.23T); 2024 (asserted by *both* Morningstar's own later reporting via secondary summaries **and** SSGA's Jan-2026 piece: "In 2024, the AUM of passive funds surpassed that of active funds"); 2025 (ICI's factbook first says "the majority (52%)" for YE2025); never, if the caliber is market cap (19% per ICI, 23% per Vanguard). The 2023-vs-2024 conflict is unresolved in my sources: Morningstar's Dec-2025 report says "back in 202_" with the year digit dropped by the PDF font subset, and I could not fetch any Morningstar page directly (all 403). **Do not state a crossover year without stating the fund universe.**

**2. ICI 52% vs Morningstar 55% vs ICI-monthly 53.7% — three "passive share of US fund assets" for essentially the same moment.** ICI factbook YE2025: 52% ($19.1T of $36.6T long-term, excl. money market and commodity ETFs). Morningstar YE2025: "over 55%" ($19.42T vs $16.02T). ICI monthly June 2026: 53.7% ($21.88T vs $18.83T, excl. funds-of-funds, includes money market treatment unclear from the fetched rendering). Drivers of the spread: whether commodity ETFs, ETNs, funds-of-funds and strategic-beta count as index; whether money market funds are in the denominator; and Morningstar's editorial "passive" tag vs ICI's "index" self-designation. **A 3pp spread on the single most-quoted number in the debate.**

**3. Morningstar's own report is internally inconsistent.** $19.42T / ($19.42T + $16.02T) = **54.8%**, but the prose says "over 55%". Minor, but it is the exact number the "majority passive" headline rests on.

**4. ICI 19% vs Vanguard 23% for market-cap ownership, same date (Dec 2024 / YE2025 adjacent).** Pure numerator definition: ICI counts **US-domiciled index domestic equity** MFs+ETFs; Vanguard counts **equity funds worldwide allocated to US equities** plus Morningstar "U.S. Equity" global-category funds, over a **CRSP Total Market** denominator. Both parties are motivated to report a *small* number, and they still differ by 4–5pp. Any third party quoting "passive owns ~20% of the US market" has picked one of these without saying which.

**5. Chinco–Sammon's "double" depends on which pair you divide.** 33.5% vs 16% is double. But 24.6% (after-hours-volume proxy) vs 16% is 1.5×; 38.5% vs 11% (ICI stripped of Vanguard's CRSP assets) is 3.5×; and 33.5% vs their own 6% (what their five-index method would yield if all passive were index-fund passive) is 5.6×. The paper is explicit that ICI's 16% "is also not a lower bound for our calculation" because the two numerators cover different index sets. **The title's "double" is a choice, not a result.**

**6. The Chinco–Sammon headline moved between drafts.** Klement (Nov 2022) cites 37.8% for end-2020; the published paper's 2020 value is 32.19% and 2021 is 33.50%. A web summarizer in this session also returned "25.9%" as an alleged daily-volume alternative and "39.8%" as an upper bound — **both are wrong**; the paper's actual alternatives are 24.6% (after-hours proxy) and 38.5% (with Vanguard CRSP added). Treat any secondary citation of this paper's numbers as suspect.

**7. Passive share of *trading* runs from 1.2% to 28% depending on what you count.** Vanguard: index funds' portfolio-management turnover = **1.2%** of US exchange volume (2024). ICI: ETF **secondary-market** trading = **28%** of US stock trading (2025). SSGA: **closing auction** = **15.39%** of US volume (Dec 2025), up from 8.40% (Jan 2018), with no passive attribution given. These are not in conflict — they are three different denominators and three different numerators — but they are routinely swapped. The bubble thesis needs the 28%/15% framing; the debunking needs the 1.2% framing. Neither side reports the other's number.

**8. S&P DJI's $13T indexed to the S&P 500 alone vs ICI's $19.1T for all index funds.** These cannot both be describing the same universe. The reconciliation is that S&P DJI's "indexed" includes non-fund vehicles worldwide (institutional mandates, internal indexing, insurance, structured products) while ICI's counts registered US funds only. That reconciliation **is** the Chinco–Sammon thesis, arriving from an index provider whose commercial incentive points the same direction. Note the incentive symmetry: index providers and active managers both benefit from a big number; index-fund giants and the fund trade association both benefit from a small one. The only source here with no obvious axe on the *size* question is the Fed paper — and it reports the small (14%, March 2020) caliber.

**9. Denominator drift in the market-cap ratios.** ICI uses World Federation of Exchanges; Vanguard uses CRSP Total Market; Chinco–Sammon use "the US stock market" (CRSP-based); Vanguard's global figure uses MSCI ACWI and concedes ACWI "targets about **85%** of global investable equities... thus **overstating** the overall ownership fraction." US total market cap moved from ~$62T (YE2024) to ~$69T (YE2025) — a 11% denominator move in one year, larger than the entire year-over-year change in most reported passive shares. **Ratios computed a year apart are not comparable without fixing the index.**

**10. The magnitude gap between the ownership number and the price-effect number is the article's real story.** The most-quoted fact is a 50–55% *asset* share. The best-identified causal estimate is **11%** more inelastic demand over 20 years (Haddad-Huebner-Loualiche, AER 2025), and the best-identified *price-impact* trend is index-add/delete abnormal returns **converging to zero** as the passive share doubled (Chinco-Sammon 2024). The Fed's May 2026 Financial Stability Report does not mention passive investing or ETFs at all. Every source that produces a large scary number measures **stocks of ownership**; every source that produces a small number measures **flows, trades, or prices**.

Gaps I could not close.** (a) No fetchable primary Morningstar page — every morningstar.com and advisorhub/thinkadvisor/cnbc URL returned 403, so the canonical $13.29T/$13.23T pair is search-index-sourced only and should be re-verified before publication.
