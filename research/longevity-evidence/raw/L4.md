# L4 — NAD+ precursors (NR / NMN): human trial evidence

Working notes. Compiled 2026-07-28. Line scope: human trial evidence only. Regulatory status
and market sizing deliberately excluded (L7). Mouse work included only where it is the *origin*
of the mechanistic claim or where it directly contradicts a human-facing claim.

---

## 0. Headline of the line

The NR/NMN evidence base is a near-textbook case of **tier-1 → tier-3 silent promotion**.
There is:

- **Strong tier-2 (pharmacokinetic/surrogate) evidence**: oral NR and NMN reliably raise
  *blood* NAD+, dose-dependently, reproducibly, in many independent labs.
- **Weak and mostly null tier-3 (clinical/functional) evidence**: essentially every
  adequately-powered human RCT with a functional primary endpoint in a general or
  metabolically-unhealthy population has been null.
- **A contested tier-0**: the founding premise — "NAD+ declines with age in humans" — was
  substantially weakened in 2025–2026 by two Nature Metabolism papers.
- **No completed-and-published RCT with a hard clinical endpoint.** The one that exists
  (NOPARK, n=410, 52 weeks, MDS-UPDRS) completed 17 June 2025 and, as of 2026-07-28, has
  *still not been published or reported*. That silence is itself the single most important
  fact on this line.

## 1. The founding premise: does NAD+ actually decline with age in humans?

### 1a. The classical citations (all small, all cross-sectional)

**Massudi et al. 2012, PLoS ONE 7(7):e42357** — "Age-Associated Changes In Oxidative Stress
and NAD+ Metabolism In Human Tissue". Human *pelvic skin* samples, n=49, ages 0–77
(newborns to 77). Negative correlation NAD+ vs age: males p=0.001, r=−0.706; females
p=0.01, r=−0.537. Caliber: **one tissue (skin), cross-sectional, post-mortem/surgical
tissue, n=49.** This is the most-cited "human NAD+ declines" datapoint and it is a skin
biopsy correlation.

**Zhu, Lu, Lee, Ugurbil & Chen 2015, PNAS 112(9):2876–2881** — "In vivo NAD assay reveals the
intracellular NAD contents and redox state in healthy human brain and their age dependences."
31P-MRS of *occipital lobe* in **n=17 total** (7 aged 21–26; 4 aged 33–36; 6 aged 59–68).
Verbatim: "an age-dependent increase of intracellular NADH and age-dependent reductions in
NAD(+), total NAD contents, and NAD(+)/NADH redox potential of the healthy human brain were
revealed in this study." Caliber: **n=17, one brain region, cross-sectional, three age bins.**
This is the entirety of the in-vivo human brain evidence for the founding claim.

**Janssens et al. 2022, Nature Aging 2:254–263 (PMID 37118369)** — "Healthy aging and muscle
function are positively associated with NAD+ abundance in humans." Muscle metabolome,
cross-sectional, comparing young adults vs exercise-trained older vs normally-active older
vs physically-impaired older. NAD+ "was one of the most prominent metabolites that was lower
in older adults"; exercise-trained older individuals had NAD+ "more similar to those found in
younger individuals". NAD+ correlated with steps/day and mitochondrial + muscle function.
**Caliber trap**: this is an association with *activity/health status*, not with age per se —
the trained older group looked young. The paper is routinely cited as "NAD+ declines with age"
when what it actually shows is "NAD+ tracks physical activity."

Related: **Regular exercise protects against aging-associated decline in skeletal muscle NAD
content** (ScienceDirect S053155652300030X) — same directional message.

### 1b. The 2026 contradiction (this is the big one)

**Trętowicz MM, Scantlebery AML, Schomakers BV, Eroglu KD, … Houtkooper RH (32 authors),
"Human whole-blood NAD+ levels do not vary with age or lifestyle interventions", Nature
Metabolism, published 14 May 2026, doi 10.1038/s42255-026-01537-5, PMID 42135539.**
Rigorously validated UHPLC–HRMS quantification of NAD+ across **seven independent human
cohorts**. Finding: whole-blood NAD+ "remain[s] remarkably stable with age and across
lifestyle interventions, but change[s] in response to nicotinamide riboside supplementation,
as expected." Authors' conclusion: the results "challenge the utility of blood NAD+ levels as
a biomarker of ageing or lifestyle factors."
Accompanied by an editorial/News-&-Views: **"Whole-blood NAD+ levels do not reflect healthy
ageing", Nature Metabolism, doi 10.1038/s42255-026-01540-w.**
Author overlap with Helsinki (Pietiläinen, Pirinen), Leiden (Slagboom, Beekman, Deelen),
Amsterdam UMC (Houtkooper), Valencia (Gomez-Cabrera).

**Consequence for the field**: essentially every consumer NAD+ test, and every trial that used
blood NAD+ as its efficacy readout, is now measuring something that does not itself track
ageing. The defence offered by industry (e.g. aboutnad.com, a vendor blog) is "blood ≠ tissue"
— which is true, and which also concedes that the surrogate everyone has been measuring is
not the thing anyone cares about.

**Vinten KT, Trętowicz MM, Coskun E, van Weeghel M, Cantó C, Zapata-Pérez R, Janssens GE,
Houtkooper RH. "NAD+ precursor supplementation in human ageing: clinical evidence and
challenges." Nature Metabolism 7(10):1974–1990, October 2025, doi 10.1038/s42255-025-01387-7.**
Verbatim from the abstract: "evidence for an age-related decline in NAD+ levels in humans has
been consistently observed only in a limited number of studies"; "although preclinical studies
support the idea that supplementation with NAD+ precursors is a promising therapeutic strategy
to promote healthy ageing, human clinical trials have shown limited efficacy"; "the published
body of data on NAD+ dynamics in human tissues remains sparse, and the extrapolation of
rodent-based data is not straightforward".
**COI note**: co-author Carles Cantó is a Nestlé Institute of Health Sciences scientist;
Nestlé subsequently ran the head-to-head precursor trial (§5).

## 2. The mouse lineage (origin of the claim) — and the mouse-level refutation

- **Zhang H, Ryu D, … Auwerx J. Science 352(6292):1436–43 (2016)** — "NAD+ repletion improves
  mitochondrial and stem cell function and enhances life span in mice." NR rejuvenated muscle
  stem cells, delayed senescence of NSC/melanocyte SC; lifespan extension **~5%**, treatment
  started in *old* (~24-month) mice. This is the single "NR extends lifespan" citation the
  whole consumer field rests on.
- **Mills KF, … Imai S. Cell Metabolism 24(6):795–806 (2016)** — 12-month NMN administration
  to chow-fed WT C57BL/6N mice during normal ageing. Suppressed age-associated body-weight
  gain, improved energy metabolism, physical activity, insulin sensitivity, plasma lipids, eye
  function. **Title claim is "mitigates age-associated physiological decline" — not lifespan.**
  The study did not demonstrate lifespan extension.
- **Gomes AP, … Sinclair DA. Cell 155(7):1624–38 (2013)** — NMN restored mitochondrial
  homeostasis in 22-month mice within ~1 week. (Origin of the "reversed aging in a week"
  press line.)

**The refutation nobody quotes:**
**Harrison DE, … Miller RA et al., Aging Cell 20(5):e13328 (2021), PMID 33788371 — NIA
Interventions Testing Program.** Title itself: "17-a-estradiol late in life extends lifespan
in aging UM-HET3 male mice; **nicotinamide riboside and three other drugs do not affect
lifespan in either sex**."
Caliber, verbatim/near-verbatim from the paper: NR chloride at **1,000 ppm in diet**, fed
**continuously from 8 months of age**, genetically heterogeneous UM-HET3 mice, **156 males and
136 females** in the treatment group across three independent sites (UT / UMich / Jackson Lab).
Result: males median lifespan **−3% (p=0.252)**, 90th percentile −3% (p=0.99); females median
**0% (p=0.612)**, 90th percentile +2% (p=0.99).
This is the most rigorous, best-powered, multi-site, blinded-to-hope lifespan test of NR that
exists, and it is flatly null. It is very rarely cited in consumer-facing NR material.

## 3. The pharmacokinetic layer — does oral NR/NMN raise NAD+ in blood, and in TISSUE?

### Blood: yes, robustly.

**Trammell SAJ, … Brenner C. Nat Commun 7:12948 (2016)** — first human PK trial of NR.
n=12 healthy non-pregnant subjects (6M/6F), ages 30–55; single morning doses of 100, 300 or
1,000 mg on three test days separated by 7-day washout (crossover of dose sequence).
1,000 mg raised PBMC NAD+ from **~12 to ~18 µM**; NAAD peaked ~8 h.
Hype seed, verbatim from the paper: "human blood NAD+ can rise as much as **2.7-fold** with a
single oral dose of NR **in a pilot study of one individual**." That n=1 number is the
ancestor of a large amount of marketing copy.
Funding: "supported by grants from the Roy J. Carver Trust, ChromaDex and National Institutes
of Health." COI: "F.J., R.W.D. and C.B. own stock in ChromaDex."

**Conze D, Brenner C, Kruger CL. Sci Rep 9:9772 (2019), PMC6611812** — largest NR RCT to date.
n=140 randomized healthy overweight adults, 40–60 y, BMI 25–30; placebo vs 100 / 300 /
1,000 mg NR (NIAGEN) daily for **8 weeks** (2-week run-in).
Whole-blood NAD+ increase at day 14: **100 mg +22 ± 9%; 300 mg +51 ± 7%; 1,000 mg +142 ± 14%.**
Clinical/functional panel measured: BP, HR, weight, lipids, CBC, liver/kidney function,
homocysteine, resting energy expenditure, BCAAs, CRP. Nothing clinically moved; the reported
findings are all safety-negative ("no reports of flushing", "did not elevate LDL-C or
dysregulate 1-carbon metabolism").
**Funding verbatim: "ChromaDex funded the study."** COI verbatim: "Claire Kruger and Dietrich
Conze are employees of ChromaDex. Charles Brenner is the inventor of intellectual property
licensed by ChromaDex and serves as their chief scientific adviser."
So the biggest NR trial in existence is a company-funded, company-authored dose-response study
whose only positive endpoint is a blood biomarker.

**Martens CR, … Seals DR. Nat Commun 9:1286 (2018), PMC5876407** — n=30 randomized, **24
completed**, ages 55–79; 2×6-week randomized double-blind placebo-controlled crossover;
500 mg × 2/day (1,000 mg/d).
NAD+ in PBMCs **~+60%** vs placebo (mean change 6.2 pmol/mg protein; one-sided 95% CI
(0.074, ∞)). NAAD rose "nearly fivefold".
BP/stiffness — the part that got the headlines: SBP mean change **−3.9 mmHg** (one-sided 95%
CI (−∞, −0.058)), DBP −2.0 mmHg, but verbatim: "these comparisons were **not statistically
significant after correction for multiple comparisons**." Carotid-femoral PWV: "a trend
towards a reduction" — also "not statistically significant after correction". The famous
"9 mmHg" figure is a **post-hoc subgroup** of participants with elevated/stage-I hypertension,
and the authors themselves wrote: "Because this post-hoc subgroup analysis was exploratory,
**no statistical inferences can be made**."
Funding verbatim: "This work was supported by NIH grants AG000279 and TR001082" AND "**Study
pills, NR standards for metabolite analyses and partial funding support were provided by
ChromaDex, Inc.**" — yet the paper states "**The authors declare no competing interests.**"
That juxtaposition is worth flagging on its own.

### Tissue: this is where the story breaks.

**Elhassan YS, … Lavery GG. Cell Reports 28(7):1717–1728 (2019), PMC6702140.**
n=**12 aged men**, 1 g NR/day for **21 days**, placebo-controlled randomized double-blind
crossover, with **muscle biopsies**.
The headline everyone quotes: "NR elevated the muscle NAD+ metabolome."
The sentence almost nobody quotes: muscle NAD+ metabolome rose **"without an increase in NAD+
(NR 210 pmol/mg versus 197 pmol/mg; p = 0.22)."**
i.e. **muscle NAD+ itself did not significantly increase.** What rose were NAAD and
nicotinamide *clearance products* — arguably a marker of disposal, not of repletion.
Functional outcomes, verbatim: "No differences were detected between the NR and placebo groups
in skeletal muscle complex I- and complex II-mediated oxidative phosphorylation"; "we did not
observe any differences in the participants' peak hand-grip strengths (NR 32.5 kg versus
placebo 34.7 kg; p = 0.96)."
Muscle RNA-seq showed **down**regulation of energy-metabolism and mitochondria pathways
(opposite direction to the mouse story). Circulating inflammatory cytokines fell — the one
consistent positive signal in the NR literature.
NR supplier verbatim: "NR was supplied as 250 mg capsules by the manufacturer (Niagen,
ChromaDex, Irvine, CA)." COI verbatim: "C.B. is the inventor of patents licensed by ChromaDex,
owns stock in ChromaDex, and serves as adviser to ChromaDex and Cytokinetics."

**Remie CME, … Schrauwen P. Am J Clin Nutr 112(2):413–426 (2020), PMID 32320006.**
n=13 healthy overweight/obese men and women, 1,000 mg/d NR for **6 weeks**, randomized
double-blind placebo-controlled crossover, with hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamps, MRS,
muscle biopsies, ex vivo mitochondrial function.
NR **did** increase skeletal muscle NAD+ metabolites and altered muscle acetylcarnitine;
minor changes in body composition and sleeping metabolic rate.
NR did **not** change: muscle mitochondrial respiration, hepatic lipid content, cardiac
function measures, inflammatory markers, blood glucose, or insulin sensitivity.

So: two independent muscle-biopsy trials. One shows the metabolome moves but NAD+ itself does
not (Elhassan); the other shows metabolites move but nothing functional does (Remie). **There
is no human trial demonstrating that oral NR raises tissue NAD+ *and* improves a tissue
function.**

## 4. Human RCTs with clinical or functional endpoints — the null list

**Dollerup OL, … Jessen N, Treebak JT. Am J Clin Nutr 108(2):343–353 (2018), PMID 29992272.**
n=40 obese, sedentary men, BMI>30, ages 40–70; **2,000 mg/d NR** (1,000 mg × 2) vs placebo,
**12 weeks**, parallel-group, double-blind. Primary outcome: insulin sensitivity by
hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp.
Verbatim results: "Insulin sensitivity, endogenous glucose production, and glucose disposal
and oxidation were not improved by NR supplementation."
Verbatim conclusion: "12 wk of NR supplementation in doses of 2000 mg/d appears safe, but does
**not** improve insulin sensitivity and whole-body glucose metabolism in obese,
insulin-resistant men."
Also null for resting energy expenditure, lipolysis, lipid oxidation, body composition.
(Note: this group at Copenhagen signed a Material Transfer Agreement with ChromaDex on
15 April 2014 — "ChromaDex will provide the University with quantities of its patented
NIAGEN™ nicotinamide riboside (NR) ingredient for use in research" — lead investigator
Jonas Thue Treebak. Same Treebak later co-authored the field's most critical review, §6.)

**Lundgren/Pietiläinen twin study — Sci Adv 9:eadd5163 (2023), PMC9839336.** Often cited as
the "NR builds mitochondria in humans" paper. Caliber: **20 BMI-discordant monozygotic twin
pairs**, dose escalated 250→1,000 mg/d over **5 months**. The placebo comparison is tiny:
"Of the concordant twin pairs, one cotwin was randomized to placebo and the other one to NR"
— **only 4 concordant pairs = 8 individuals** carry the placebo control; the 16 discordant
pairs both received NR. Positive: NAD+ metabolism, muscle mitochondrial number, myoblast
differentiation, gut microbiota, DNA methylation.
Negative, verbatim: NR "did not ameliorate adiposity or metabolic health"; in fact "body
weight (~3 kg) and the whole-body fat percentage increased significantly" and there were
"elevated fasting glucose and insulin levels and impaired insulin sensitivity"; and "NR's
effect on body composition and metabolic health did not markedly differ from the effects of
placebo." A 5-month NR arm in which people gained ~3 kg of fat is routinely marketed as the
mitochondrial-biogenesis proof.

**NR in long-COVID — 24-week double-blind placebo-controlled RCT, PMC12675013 (2025/2026).**
n=58 completed baseline; 2,000 mg/d NR; 2:1 randomization (NR-NR vs PBO-NR sequence), 20 weeks
NR or 10 weeks placebo then 10 weeks NR. Primary outcomes: cognition (ECog, RBANS, TMT-B) and
NAD+.
NAD+ rose **2.6–3.1-fold** by 5–10 weeks (placebo 0.93–1.0-fold).
Verbatim: "No significant between-group differences were observed for cognitive outcomes
(ECog, RBANS, TMT-B; p-values = 0.47–0.74)." "There were no significant differences in fatigue
severity (p = 0.59), sleep quality (p = 0.69), and symptoms of anxiety (p = 0.84) or depression
(p = 0.20) between PBO and NR groups."
Verbatim conclusion: "In individuals with long-COVID, NR was safe and effective in increasing
NAD+ levels within 5 weeks. However, NAD+ augmentation did not produce statistically
significant between-group differences (NR vs. PBO) in objective cognitive outcomes or in
self-reported measures of fatigue severity, sleep quality, or mood."
Post-hoc: pooling all NR exposure "showed improvements in executive function, fatigue, sleep,
and depression after 10 weeks" — i.e. the uncontrolled within-person comparison is positive
while the randomized comparison is null. Classic.
Funding: **Niagen Bioscience funded and donated NR/placebo via ChromaDex; two authors hold
equity in Niagen Bioscience.**

**NICE trial — McDermott MM, et al. "Nicotinamide riboside for peripheral artery disease: the
NICE randomized clinical trial." Nat Commun 15 (2024), PMID 38871717.** (Publisher Correction:
PMID 39134546.) NCT03743636; sponsor Northwestern University, collaborator American Heart
Association. n=90 with lower-extremity PAD; 6 months; three arms: NR 1,000 mg + resveratrol
125 mg; NR 1,000 mg + placebo; double placebo. Primary outcome: 6-minute walk distance at 6 mo.
Registry-posted ITT results (results first posted 14 Jan 2025), least-squares mean change in
6MWD: NR+resveratrol **−6.93 ± 7.84 m** (n=33); NR alone **+7.00 ± 8.69 m** (n=28); placebo
**−10.58 ± 8.45 m** (n=29). NR vs placebo between-group difference **+17.57 m, one-sided 90%
CI lower bound 1.77, p=0.0775**. NR+resveratrol vs placebo **+3.65 m, p=0.3762**.
**The trial was declared positive against a pre-specified ONE-SIDED p<0.10 threshold.** Under a
conventional two-sided α=0.05 it is null. The emphasized effect (31.0 m) is from the
**≥75%-adherence per-protocol subgroup (n=60)**. For context, minimal clinically important
difference for 6MWD in PAD is generally taken as ~20 m — the ITT estimate sits below it.
Authors' own line: "a larger clinical trial to confirm these findings is needed."
This is, as of now, **the strongest functional-endpoint NR result in a common disease, and it
is a one-sided-p<0.10, n=90, sub-MCID result.**

### NMN side

**Yoshino M, … Klein S, Yoshino J. Science 372(6547):1224–1229 (2021), PMID 33888596.**
n=**25** postmenopausal women with prediabetes, overweight/obese (NMN n=13, placebo n=12);
**250 mg/day NMN, 10 weeks**, randomized double-blind placebo-controlled.
Verbatim: "Insulin-stimulated glucose disposal, assessed by using the hyperinsulinemic-
euglycemic clamp, and skeletal muscle insulin signaling [phosphorylation of protein kinase
AKT and mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR)] increased after NMN supplementation."
The widely-quoted "25% improvement in muscle insulin sensitivity" comes from here. What did
NOT change: body weight, body composition, and the other metabolic endpoints.
**The contradiction, from a commercial rival:** Brenner C. "Comment on 'Nicotinamide
mononucleotide increases muscle insulin sensitivity in prediabetic women'." Science 373(6553)
(2021), PMID 34326206. Argument: the trial was **not effectively randomized** — hepatic lipid
content was 6.3 ± 1.2% in the NMN arm vs 14.8 ± 2.0% in placebo (**P = 0.003**); since a
principal target of NMN is liver fat and fatty liver depresses muscle insulin sensitivity, "one
cannot reasonably expect that the results reported would be reproduced in an effectively
randomized trial."
**Charles Brenner is the NR patent inventor, ChromaDex stockholder and ChromaDex chief
scientific adviser** — i.e. the field's most prominent methodological critique of the flagship
NMN trial was written by the person who commercially benefits from NR beating NMN. Both the
critique and the COI should be reported.
Response: Klein S, Yoshino M. Science (2021), PMID 34326209 — baseline muscle insulin
sensitivity was identical between groups; muscle insulin signaling rose only in NMN; baseline
IHTG imbalance "do[es] not negate the effects of NMN observed in muscle."

**Igarashi M, … Yamauchi T. npj Aging 8:5 (2022), PMID 35927255, PMC9158788.**
n=42 healthy men **aged ≥65**, 250 mg/day NMN vs placebo, **12 weeks**, parallel-group
randomized double-blind.
Whole-blood NAD+ and NAD+-related metabolites rose significantly.
Functional: gait speed improved; **left**-hand grip strength improved; **right**-hand grip
strength did **not**; skeletal muscle mass unchanged. A left-but-not-right grip result in a
2-arm n=42 trial is a multiplicity artifact, not a finding.
Funder: **Mitsubishi** (Mitsubishi Corporation Life Sciences) — reported by NutraIngredients
as a "Mitsubishi-funded trial".

**Yi L, et al. GeroScience 45:29–43 (2023), PMID 36482258; NCT04823260 / CTRI/2021/03/032421.**
n=80 healthy adults 40–65 y; placebo vs NMN **300 / 600 / 900 mg/day for 60 days**;
multicentre, double-blind, parallel-group, dose-dependent.
All NMN doses raised blood NAD+; six-minute-walk performance improved, most at 600 and 900 mg;
authors conclude efficacy "reaches highest at a dose of 600 mg daily oral intake."
Sponsor is an NMN manufacturer (Effepharm / Abinopharm-associated); trial is heavily used in
NMN marketing. Treat the 6MWD claim as sponsor-run and unreplicated.

**Katayoshi T, et al. Sci Rep 13:2786 (2023), PMC9935856.**
n=36 healthy middle-aged; **125 mg NMN × 2/day (250 mg/d) for 12 weeks**, randomized
double-blind placebo-controlled parallel-group.
Primary endpoint arterial stiffness (pulse wave velocity): verbatim "Pulse wave velocity values
indicating arterial stiffness tended to decrease in the NMN intake group. However, **no
significant difference was found between the two groups**" (**p = 0.097**).
The positive result circulated in media ("NMN reverses blood vessel aging") is a **post-hoc
subgroup** of participants with above-average BMI or blood glucose.
Funding verbatim: "This work was supported by a research grant from the DHC Corporation."
**All authors are employed by DHC Corporation Laboratories** (an NMN seller) — and the paper
states "The authors declare no competing interests."

## 5. Head-to-head PK, 2026

**Christen S, et al. "The differential impact of three different NAD+ boosters on circulatory
NAD and microbial metabolism in humans." Nature Metabolism 8:62–73 (2026), published
15 January 2026, doi 10.1038/s42255-025-01421-8, PMID 41540253.**
Randomized, **open-label**, placebo-controlled, **n=65** healthy participants, **1,000 mg/day**
of NR, NMN or nicotinamide (Nam) vs placebo for **14 days**.
Findings: NR and NMN "comparably increase circulatory NAD+ concentrations"; Nam does not
sustain the increase (acute/transient only) and raised homocysteine. Mechanistic twist:
ex vivo fermentation with human microbiota shows **NR and NMN are converted by gut microbiota
to nicotinic acid (NA)**, and the authors propose the systemic NAD+ rise runs through the
**Preiss–Handler pathway via microbially-generated NA**, not via intact NR/NMN reaching
tissues. Both also raised short-chain fatty acids.
Conducted and funded by the **Nestlé Institute of Health Sciences**. Accompanying commentary:
"The microbiome at the centre of NAD+ supplementation", Nat Metab, doi 10.1038/s42255-025-01438-z.
**Implication that undercuts a decade of marketing**: if the active species is microbially
produced nicotinic acid, then the "NR is uniquely bioavailable / NMN is the superior precursor"
distinction — the entire basis of the NR-vs-NMN commercial war — is largely mooted, and cheap
niacin becomes the relevant comparator. Note it is open-label and 14 days.

## 6. The systematic reviews and meta-analyses — verbatim bottom lines

**Damgaard MV & Treebak JT. "What is really known about the effects of nicotinamide riboside
supplementation in humans." Science Advances 9(29):eadi4862, 21 July 2023, PMID 37478182.**
Novo Nordisk Foundation Center for Basic Metabolic Research, University of Copenhagen.
Reviewed **25 published human NR research articles**.
Verbatim: "Collectively, oral nicotinamide riboside supplementation has displayed **few
clinically relevant effects**, and there is an **unfortunate tendency in the literature to
exaggerate the importance and robustness of reported effects**. Even so, nicotinamide riboside
may play a role in the reduction of inflammatory states and has shown some potential in the
treatment of diverse severe diseases."
Note: Treebak's own group received NIAGEN from ChromaDex under a 2014 MTA — the critic is
himself downstream of the sponsor, which strengthens rather than weakens the critique.

**Prokopidis K, et al. "The Effect of Nicotinamide Mononucleotide and Riboside on Skeletal
Muscle Mass and Function: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis." J Cachexia Sarcopenia Muscle
(2025), PMID 40275690, PMC12022230.**
**10 RCTs**, participants mean age 60.9–83; NMN 250–2,000 mg/d for 3–24 weeks (6 RCTs);
NR 500–1,000 mg/d for 3–12 weeks (4 RCTs).
Skeletal Muscle Index: "NMN supplementation showed no significant effects on SMI (n = 3; mean
difference (MD): −0.42, 95% confidence interval (CI): −0.99 – 0.14)". Handgrip strength: "no
significant differences between NMN and placebo". Gait speed: "no significant changes between
groups were observed in relation to gait speed".
Verbatim conclusion: "**Current evidence does not support NMN and NR supplementation for
preserving muscle mass and function in adults with mean age of over 60 years.**"

**"Effects of Nicotinamide Mononucleotide on Glucose and Lipid Metabolism in Adults: A
Systematic Review and Meta-analysis of Randomised Controlled Trials." Curr Diab Rep (2025),
published online Nov 2024, PMID 39531138, PMC11557618.**
**8 RCTs, n=342** middle-aged/older adults (49% female, mostly non-diabetic), NMN
**250–2,000 mg/d for 14 days to 12 weeks**, trials published 2021–2023.
No significant benefit on fasting glucose, fasting insulin, HbA1c, HOMA-IR, or lipid profile.
Verbatim conclusion: "**Short-term supplementation of NMN of 250-2000 mg/d did not show
significantly positive impacts on glucose control and lipid profile.**"

**"Efficacy of oral nicotinamide mononucleotide supplementation on glucose and lipid metabolism
for adults: a systematic review with meta-analysis on randomized controlled trials." Crit Rev
Food Sci Nutr (2024), doi 10.1080/10408398.2024.2387324.** (paywalled to me; secondary
reporting) — random-effects meta-analysis found a significant effect of NMN on **raising blood
NAD**, while "most of the clinically relevant outcomes were **not** significantly different
between NMN supplementation and control group."

**Vinten et al., Nat Metab 2025 (§1b)** — "human clinical trials have shown limited efficacy."

Pattern: **every independent synthesis published 2023–2026 says the same thing.** The blood
biomarker moves; the clinical outcomes do not.

- Yoshino 2021 (Science) — this is the notable exception: NIH/NIDDK-supported academic trial
  at WashU, which is precisely why it is the most-attacked one.

**The load-bearing point**: the two large, clean, ChromaDex-funded NR trials (Conze n=140;
Martens n=24) exist to establish *biomarker* effects and *safety*. The trials that tested a
clinical endpoint with a hard design and adequate control (Dollerup, Remie, Elhassan,
long-COVID) are null. The one positive functional trial (NICE/PAD) is academically sponsored
(Northwestern/AHA) and required a one-sided p<0.10 to be called positive.

## 8. Safety and the cancer question

**Ma X, … Goun EA. "A bioluminescent-based probe for in vivo non-invasive monitoring of
nicotinamide riboside uptake reveals a link between metastasis and NAD+ metabolism."
Biosensors and Bioelectronics 220:114826, 15 January 2023 (online Nov 2022), PMID 36371959.**
University of Missouri / EPFL, led by Elena Goun. Developed a bioluminescent NR-uptake probe
(BiNR). Found triple-negative breast cancer has high NR uptake; **NR supplementation increased
cancer prevalence and dramatically increased the rate of brain metastasis in a TNBC mouse
model.**
**Authors' own caveat, verbatim from the university/ScienceDaily release: "These findings have
not been studied in humans"** and the work "was performed in small animal models."
Funding: European Research Council (ERC-2019-COG, 866338), Swiss National Science Foundation
(51NF40_185898), NCCR Chemical Biology. (Note: **not** a 2024 paper, and **not** Missouri-only
— the correct date is Nov 2022 online / Jan 2023 in print, and the group is
Missouri + EPFL. Any source dating it 2024 is repeating a downstream error.)

Counter-direction, same tier: an NAD+ precursor **suppressed** hepatocellular cancer
progression in mice (PMC10055624); NMN promoted pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma progression
via the senescence-associated secretory phenotype in an oncogenic mouse model; high-dose
**nicotinamide** (a different molecule) reduced new keratinocyte skin cancers in the ONTRAC
prevention RCT in humans.

Honest summary: the cancer question is **tier-1 only, bidirectional, and context-dependent
(tumour type, disease stage)**. There is no human RCT powered for cancer incidence with NR or
NMN, and none is running. The correct statement is "biologically plausible, unresolved, and
untested in humans" — not "NR causes cancer" and not "NR is proven safe long-term."

Other safety notes: NR-SAFE showed 3,000 mg/day for 30 days produced no moderate/severe AEs
and **no methyl-donor depletion**, though NR recipients had a slight initial rise in serum
homocysteine. Conze 2019 found no LDL-C elevation and no 1-carbon dysregulation at
≤1,000 mg/day × 8 weeks. Longest controlled human exposure on record is the twin study
(5 months) and the long-COVID trial (20 weeks NR) — nobody has controlled human data past
~6 months.

## 9. The strongest clinical signals — Parkinson's and Ataxia Telangiectasia (reported fairly)

### Parkinson's

**NADPARK — Brakedal B, Dölle C, … Tzoulis C. Cell Metabolism 34(3):396–407 (2022),
PMID 35235774; NCT03816020.**
Double-blind randomized **phase I**, n=**30** newly diagnosed, treatment-naive PD patients,
**1,000 mg NR/day for 30 days**.
NR was well tolerated and produced a "significant, but variable" increase in **cerebral NAD**
by 31P-MRS, plus related CSF metabolites. NR recipients with increased brain NAD showed
altered cerebral metabolism on 18F-FDG-PET "associated with mild clinical improvement".
Blood/muscle transcriptomics: upregulation of mitochondrial, lysosomal and proteasomal genes.
Decreased inflammatory cytokines in serum and CSF.
**Caliber**: n=30, 30 days, phase I, **not powered for clinical outcome**; "mild clinical
improvement" is exploratory. Two authors filed a patent on NR use in Parkinson's disease.
This is the first and essentially only demonstration that an oral NAD+ precursor raises NAD in
a human *organ of interest* — and even here the response was variable across individuals.

**NR-SAFE — Nature Communications 14:5015/7913 (Nov 2023), PMC10684646; NCT05344404.**
Single-centre randomized double-blind placebo-controlled **phase I**, n=**20** PD patients,
**NR 1,500 mg twice daily = 3,000 mg/day for 4 weeks**.
Primary outcome: safety (frequency of moderate/severe AEs). Result: no moderate or severe AEs;
no significant difference in mild AEs. Blood NAD+ rose **up to 5-fold**. Slight initial rise in
serum homocysteine but methyl-donor pool integrity preserved. Secondary: "NR therapy was
associated with clinical improvement of total MDS-UPDRS scores" — **n=20, 4 weeks, secondary
endpoint, phase I; this cannot support an efficacy claim.**

**NOPARK — NCT03568968. THE key trial, and the key gap.**
Registry facts (clinicaltrials.gov API, verified 2026-07-28): lead sponsor Haukeland
University Hospital with 10 Norwegian hospital collaborators; **actual enrollment 410**;
start 6 May 2020; **completion 17 June 2025**; overall status **Completed** (verified Sept
2025); **results NOT posted**. Design: NR **500 mg twice daily vs placebo for 52 weeks**,
12 Norwegian sites. Primary outcome: "Total score on the Movement Disorders Society Unified
Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (MDS-UPDRS), assessed from baseline through 52 weeks of
treatment." Brief summary verbatim: "NOPARK is a double-blinded randomized controlled phase II
trial, with the aim to assess the efficacy of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD)-
replenishment therapy in the form of oral nicotinamide riboside (NR) in delaying the
progression of early Parkinson's disease (PD)." (Registered as phase II; widely described as
phase III.)
Also: NOPARK Open-Label Extension, NCT05546567 (NR 1,200 mg/day).
Status as of 2026-07-28: results were publicly expected "by end of 2025" then "early 2026";
**I could find no publication, preprint, conference report, registry results posting, or
sponsor topline release.** Niagen Bioscience's Q1 2026 (6 May 2026) results release does not
mention NOPARK outcomes. Q2 2026 call scheduled 4 August 2026.
Meanwhile **Niagen Bioscience secured worldwide exclusive commercial rights** to develop NR
(Niagen) as a Parkinson's therapy from Haukeland University Hospital — a licence taken *before*
public disclosure of the result.
**This is the single most consequential fact on this line.** The largest and longest NAD+
precursor RCT ever run, with a genuine clinical progression endpoint, has been sitting
unreported for over 13 months while the compound is sold at scale.

Follow-ons: **N-DOSE (NCT05589766)** dose-optimization in PD; **N-DOSE AD (NCT05617508)** in
Alzheimer's — both dose-finding, both surrogate-led.

### Ataxia Telangiectasia

**Veenhuis SJG, et al. "Nicotinamide Riboside Improves Ataxia Scores and Immunoglobulin Levels
in Ataxia Telangiectasia." Movement Disorders 36(12):2951–2957 (2021), PMID 34515380.**
**Open-label, proof-of-concept, no placebo arm**, n=**24** A-T patients, NR for **4 consecutive
months**. Mean total SARA decreased by 2.4 points and ICARS by 10.1 points during treatment.
**After NR withdrawal, ataxia scores worsened** (the withdrawal rebound is the most persuasive
element of the design). Mean serum IgG rose by 0.52 g/L in immunodeficient patients. Untargeted
metabolomics showed increased plasma NR metabolites and purine nucleosides. Well tolerated.

**Presterud R, Deng WH, Wennerström AB, et al. "Long-Term Nicotinamide Riboside Use Improves
Coordination and Eye Movements in Ataxia Telangiectasia." Movement Disorders 39(2):360–369
(2024).**
**Single-arm, open-label**, n=**10** (7 children, 3 adults; classic and variant A-T), ages
3–28, **10–37 mg/kg/day NR for 2 years**. Safe; improvements in motor coordination and eye
movements **relative to historical controls**.

**Fair reading**: A-T is where the biology is most defensible (ATM-deficiency → NAD+ depletion
→ impaired DNA repair/mitophagy) and where the clinical signal is most consistent. But
**n=24 open-label + n=10 single-arm-vs-historical-controls is not a randomized efficacy
evidence base**, and A-T ataxia scales are noisy and rater-dependent in children. The
ChromaDex/Niagen press release (15 Nov 2023) marketing this as "Phase II clinical study
demonstrates … improving motor coordination and eye movement" is the promotional layer on top.
The A-T Society (UK) maintains a periodically updated summary (most recent March 2026), which
is the right independent reference point for families.

## 10. Gaps — searched for and absent

1. **No completed, published RCT of NR or NMN with a hard clinical outcome** (mortality, MACE,
   incident disease, disability) in any population. Zero.
2. **NOPARK results do not exist in the public record** as of 2026-07-28, 13+ months after
   completion, despite being the field's flagship. No registry results, no publication, no
   topline press release.
3. **No RCT of NR or NMN longer than ~6 months with a control group.** Longest controlled human
   exposures found: NOPARK 52 weeks (unreported), twin study 5 months (near-uncontrolled),
   long-COVID 20 weeks, Presterud 2 years (single-arm, uncontrolled).
4. **No human trial showing tissue NAD+ repletion coupled to a functional improvement in that
   tissue.** Elhassan measured muscle NAD+ and it did not rise; Remie raised muscle metabolites
   and nothing functional moved; NADPARK raised brain NAD but was phase I.
5. **No human study of NAD+ precursors and cancer incidence.** The mouse signal (both
   directions) has never been tested in humans and no trial is registered to do so.
6. **No independent (non-industry-funded, non-industry-supplied) large NR trial.** Searching
   the funding statements of the major NR trials, I could not find one of substantial size that
   was free of ChromaDex product donation, funding, or an author with ChromaDex equity/advisory
   ties. NICE/PAD (Northwestern + AHA) is the closest.
7. **No NMN phase 3 trial with a clinical endpoint anywhere in clinicaltrials.gov.** A query
   for NMN + PHASE3 returned exactly one record (NCT06739720, CP003 for CFS/ME or post-COVID
   fatigue, n=130, Univ. Hong Kong, not yet recruiting as of the record).
8. **No published replication of Yoshino 2021.** Five years on, the single most-cited NMN
   clinical result — muscle insulin sensitivity in prediabetic postmenopausal women, n=25 —
   has not been independently reproduced, and the meta-analyses that include it find no
   pooled glycaemic effect.
9. **No trial has pre-registered and reported a functional endpoint as primary and hit it
   two-sided.** The nearest miss (NICE) used one-sided p<0.10.
10. **No head-to-head of NR/NMN against plain niacin/nicotinic acid on a clinical endpoint** —
    which the Nestlé 2026 mechanism (microbial conversion to NA) makes the obvious comparator.

## 11. Cross-caliber traps to flag in the article

- **"NAD+ declines ~50% by age 50"** — this number does not have a clean primary source in
  humans. What exists: Massudi 2012 (skin, n=49, correlation), Zhu 2015 (occipital brain,
  n=17), Janssens 2022 (muscle, activity-confounded). Whole-blood NAD+ is now shown *not* to
  decline with age across seven cohorts (Trętowicz 2026). Anyone quoting a single percentage
  is fusing incompatible tissues and study designs.
- **"NR raises NAD+ by 2.7-fold"** — that is an **n=1 pilot, single dose**, from Trammell 2016.
  The controlled 12-subject number is ~12→18 µM PBMC NAD+ at 1,000 mg. The controlled n=140
  number is +142 ± 14% at 1,000 mg/day (Conze 2019).
- **"NR raises muscle NAD+"** — Elhassan 2019 explicitly did not: NR 210 vs placebo 197 pmol/mg,
  p=0.22. The metabolome rose; NAD+ did not.
- **"NR lowers blood pressure by 9 mmHg"** — post-hoc exploratory subgroup of Martens 2018
  (n=24 completed); the authors wrote "no statistical inferences can be made." The
  pre-specified whole-group result was −3.9 mmHg and not significant after multiplicity
  correction.
- **"NMN improves insulin sensitivity 25%"** — n=13 vs n=12, one endpoint, and the arms were
  imbalanced on hepatic fat (6.3% vs 14.8%, P=0.003), a variable that itself drives muscle
  insulin sensitivity.
- **"NR extends lifespan"** — one mouse study, ~5%, treatment started at ~24 months (Zhang 2016).
  The ITP replication at 1,000 ppm from 8 months, n=156M/136F, three sites, found −3%/0%
  (p=0.252/0.612).
- **"NMN improves grip strength in older men"** — Igarashi 2022 improved *left* grip and not
  *right* in an n=42 trial.
- **"NR improves walking in PAD"** — NICE trial ITT +17.6 m, judged positive against a
  pre-specified **one-sided p<0.10**; the 31 m figure is the ≥75%-adherence per-protocol
  subgroup.
- **Phase labelling**: NOPARK is registered on clinicaltrials.gov as **phase II** but is
  publicly and repeatedly described (including by the sponsor's licensee) as **phase III**.
- **Date error in circulation**: the NR/brain-metastasis mouse study is frequently dated 2024;
  it is Biosens Bioelectron 220:114826, online Nov 2022, print 15 Jan 2023.
