## 1. NMN: THE US REGULATORY REVERSAL

### Timeline (assembled)
| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| ~2017 | NMN marketed in the US as a dietary supplement (the fact FDA eventually accepted in 2025) |
| 2019-2021 | Metro International Biotech LLC develops MIB-626, a proprietary crystalline β-NMN, as a **drug**. David Sinclair is co-founder / head of its Scientific Advisory Board. IND filed (date is non-public — this is itself a policy problem). |
| 10–11 Nov 2022 | FDA concludes NMN is excluded from the dietary-supplement definition under FD&C Act §201(ff)(3)(B) ("drug preclusion" / "race to market"). Publicly surfaced by **ChromaDex press release, 11 Nov 2022** — ChromaDex sells the *competing* NAD precursor NR (Tru Niagen). |
| start of 2023 | Amazon delists NMN dietary supplements. |
| 6–7 Mar 2023 | NPA + Alliance for Natural Health USA file citizen petition (FDA received 6 Mar 2023) asking FDA to reverse or exercise enforcement discretion, to explain its interpretation, and to disclose the IND date relied on. CRN files a separate petition on drug preclusion generally. |
| 9 Jan 2024 | ChromaDex files with FDA calling the exclusion **"the correct"** determination; says pursuit of NMN as a drug was "overtly evident based on publicly available information"; calls industry claims of ignorance **"patently unfounded"** and the innovation-stifling argument **"disingenuous."** Markets NR as a **"superior"** alternative. |
| 28 Aug 2024 | NPA files complaint against FDA in **US District Court for the District of Columbia**. Metro International Biotech intervenes/argues it invested years and millions developing NMN as a drug. |
| Oct 2024 | **Judge Paul L. Friedman** stays proceedings pending FDA's answer to the citizen petition. |
| Jul 2025 | FDA tells trade press it is delayed answering the petition — reason given: short staffing. |
| **29 Sep 2025** | **FDA issues two letters** (docket ref seen: **FDA-2023-P-0872-2754**), granting in part / denying in part the NPA-ANH petition and the CRN petition. Conclusion: **NMN is not excluded from the definition of a dietary supplement** under §201(ff)(3)(B). Reasoning: evidence NMN was marketed as a dietary supplement in the US as early as 2017, before the IND authorization date; and — the key doctrinal move — FDA **no longer requires that the prior marketing have been *lawful***. Venable's summary of FDA's language: FDA does not require "proof that the ingredient was lawfully marketed or otherwise compliant with all laws." |
| ~Oct 2025 | NMN products reappear on Amazon. NPA voluntarily dismisses its lawsuit **without prejudice**. |
| **2 Dec 2025** | FDA sends letters to **SyncoZymes** and **Inner Mongolia Kingdomway Pharmaceutical Ltd** setting aside the 2022 determinations against their NDI notifications — NDI status reinstated. |
| 10 Dec 2025 | NPA press release. Daniel Fabricant: "FDA's recent letters represent a massive U-turn, and we're proud that we were able to help shape the outcome." |
| Feb 2026 → Jul 2026 | Position reported as still in force. **Answer to the brief's question: YES — as of 2026 NMN is legally sellable as a dietary supplement in the US.** |

### What FDA did NOT concede (the part the industry press buried)
CRN (Steve Mister, President & CEO): "FDA missed a pivotal opportunity to bring clarity and balance to the line between pharmaceuticals and dietary supplements." Megan Olsen (CRN GC): "The same unstable framework remains in place for the next ingredient, inviting repeat uncertainty."

Five specific CRN objections to the 29 Sep 2025 decision:
1. FDA still uses **confidential IND effective dates** as the trigger — industry cannot know when the clock started.
2. "Substantial clinical investigations" left undefined; even early-phase/limited studies can preclude an ingredient.
3. **US-only marketing evidence**; foreign prior marketing rejected.
4. FDA refused interpretive guidance or rulemaking, citing **limited resources**.
5. Structural problem untouched.

Other practitioner reactions (nutraingredients, 8 Oct 2025):
- Rend Al-Mondhiry (Amin Wasserman Gurnani): FDA's interpretation of "substantial clinical investigations" "seems to broaden application of the drug preclusion clause"; "a mixed bag."
- Scott Bass (Sidley Austin): FDA's use of "substantial" is "100% wrong," but the letter is "a major shift in their approach to DSHEA issues."
- Loren Israelsen (UNPA): "FDA seems to be protecting the incentives if they decide an N of one for a very interesting drug candidate gets the benefit of the doubt."

AHPA (blog, 1 Oct 2025): FDA "declined to address other unresolved concerns" about drug preclusion; AHPA will keep seeking "greater clarity, certainty, and fairness regarding the path to market for dietary ingredients."

### Still required to actually sell
Reversal ≠ free pass. A **New Dietary Ingredient Notification (NDIN)** is still required unless sourcing from a manufacturer with an accepted NDIN. NDIN acceptance is **notifier-specific** (specific production process and conditions of use) — not a blanket ingredient authorization. FDA also did **not** allow self-affirmed GRAS as a substitute.

### COI map for this fight
- **ChromaDex / Niagen Bioscience** sells NR; benefited directly from NMN being off the market; actively filed in support of the exclusion.
- **Metro International Biotech** holds the drug program (MIB-626) whose IND triggered preclusion; **David Sinclair** co-founded it / chairs its SAB — i.e. the field's most prominent public NMN advocate was commercially positioned on the *drug* side of the wall that removed NMN from supplement shelves.
- **NPA / ANH / CRN / AHPA / UNPA** are supplement-industry trade bodies; the reversal is worth money to their members.

## 3. DSHEA: WHAT MAY BE CLAIMED

Framework (FD&C Act §403(r)(6); 21 CFR 101.93): a supplement may make **structure/function** claims (effect on the structure or function of the body, general well-being, nutrient-deficiency disease) **without premarket FDA approval**, provided the firm notifies FDA within 30 days and carries the disclaimer. It may **not** claim to diagnose, treat, cure, mitigate or prevent a **disease** — that makes the product an unapproved new drug. *(I could not fetch eCFR — redirected to unblock.federalregister.gov. Statutory text unverified in-session; flagged as a gap.)*

The disclaimer, as it actually appears in the wild (verbatim from blueprint.bryanjohnson.com, fetched 2026-07-28):
> "These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."

**Operational consequence for this article:** "slows aging," "supports healthy aging," "supports cellular energy" are permitted structure/function territory. "Reverses Alzheimer's," "prevents cancer" are disease claims and trigger enforcement. **Aging itself is not a recognized disease indication**, which is exactly why the whole sector can market to it while the FDA-enforceable line sits somewhere else. Enforcement therefore lands almost entirely on companies that stray into *disease* claims — not on companies making sweeping *aging* claims.

### Related enforcement: NAD+ IV
- GenoGenix NAD+ Injection, 100 mg/mL and 200 mg/mL, 10 mL multi-dose amber vials, **Lot GG121624-023**. Voluntary recall initiated **30 Jul 2025**; **classified Class I by FDA on 21 Oct 2025**. ## 4. ADVERTISING SELF-REGULATION — THE MOST IMPORTANT 2026 RULING

**BBB National Programs, National Advertising Division (NAD), Case #7487 — decision 16 Mar 2026.**
- Advertiser: **Niagen Bioscience, Inc.** (formerly ChromaDex) — Tru Niagen (nicotinamide riboside).
- Challenger: **Reus Research LLC** — a *competitor*. (Competitor-v-competitor; note that NAD also ruled on Reus's own "Cata Kor" NAD claims on 13 Oct 2025, finding some supported and recommending others be modified/discontinued.)
- NAD recommended discontinuing/modifying: the establishment claim "clinically proven to significantly increase NAD+ levels, and help maintain them with daily use"; organ-specific claims ("supports heart health," "supports brain health"); broader vitality/anti-aging claims; and consumer testimonials conveying unsupported health improvements.
- Niagen: "will appeal NAD's decision."

**National Advertising Review Board (NARB) panel, Case #7487-346 — decision 21 May 2026.** Panel upheld:
- On establishment claims: the clinical studies "did not support the claim asserting that specified increases in NAD+ levels following use of Tru Niagen had been clinically proven for the general population."
- **The doctrinal holding that matters for the whole field:** "the required substantiation depends on the message conveyed to consumers, not the advertiser's regulatory classification." Many claims "went beyond cell-level mechanistic effects to promise perceptible or functional health benefits that were not properly supported."
- Discontinue: "clinically proven" establishment claims; heart, brain and immune health claims; anti-aging claims and testimonials showing real-world improvement; muscle health and energy claims promising functional recovery or vitality.
- Niagen advertiser statement: "deeply disappointed with the NARB Panel's decision but will nevertheless comply."
- Niagen also warned the decision "has a chilling effect on innovation and may actively discourage future investments in human clinical trials."

**This is the tier-1→tier-3 promotion, adjudicated.** A self-regulatory body with no statutory power nonetheless articulated the exact rule this article is about: *raising a biomarker is not evidence of a benefit a person can feel.*

**Class action:** a false-advertising class action alleges **Niagen Bioscience, Inc. and ChromaDex, Inc.** marketed Tru Niagen NR as delivering anti-aging, cellular repair and organ health benefits via raised NAD+, "overstated and misrepresented clinical findings," and charged a price premium for "snake oil" supplements. (Reported by classaction.org; complaint not fetched — case number/court unverified. GAP.)

### FTC — older but the only actual government consumer-protection hits on "anti-aging"
- **Telomerase Activation Sciences, Inc. and Noel Thomas Patton (TA Sciences)** — FTC complaint **Feb 2018**; **final consent order approved 19 Apr 2018** (matter 142-3103, C-4644). Products: **TA-65MD** (capsule/powder) and **TA-65 for Skin**. FTC alleged the company lacked scientific evidence for a broad range of anti-aging/health claims (prevent and repair DNA damage, restore aging immune systems, increase bone density, reverse effects of aging skin and eyes, prevent or reduce cancer risk). Order also barred misrepresenting paid advertising as independent programming and failing to disclose material connections with endorsers.
- **Quantum Wellness Botanical Institute, LLC; CEO Fred Auzene; marketer Maria Gutierrez Veloso — "ReJuvenation"** — settlements announced **5 Feb 2020**, judgments totalling ~$3.4M, **$660,000** paid for consumer redress. Ads claimed the pill would reverse aging and treat heart-attack damage, heart disease, blindness, brain damage from stroke, Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, deafness.
- **Nothing found**: no FTC action 2024–2026 against an NMN/NAD+ longevity supplement marketer. The 2024–2026 action in this category is *self-regulatory* (NAD/NARB) and *private* (class action), not governmental.

## 5. LOYAL (Cellular Longevity, Inc.) — WHAT WAS ACTUALLY GRANTED

**LOY-001 (large/giant breeds; mechanism: reduce IGF-1 toward small-breed levels)**
- **28 Nov 2023**: FDA CVM accepted "the technical effectiveness portion of our conditional approval application for LOY-001's use in large dog lifespan extension." Submission was **2,300+ pages**, comprising interventional studies plus an **observational study of 451 dogs**.
- Loyal's own framing: "From our data, the FDA believes LOY-001 is likely to be effective for large dog lifespan extension." Safety and manufacturing sections still required; after conditional approval a **confirmatory pivotal lifespan study must run in parallel**.

**LOY-002 (senior dogs; caloric-restriction-mimetic framing; daily pill)**
- **26 Feb 2025**: FDA CVM accepted **Reasonable Expectation of Effectiveness (RXE)**. Loyal's own post is explicit: this is **"NOT approval."** Two technical sections (manufacturing, safety) still outstanding at that time.
- STAY study as described **Feb 2025**: **1,000 dogs**, aged **≥10 years**, weighing **≥14 lb**.
- **13 Jan 2026**: FDA CVM accepted the **Target Animal Safety (TAS)** section of the **Expanded Conditional Approval (XCA)** application. Now **2 of 3 major technical sections** accepted; **Chemistry, Manufacturing and Controls (CMC) remains**. Safety package: data from multiple studies, **>400 dogs** dosed for varying durations **including up to one year**, at **1×, 3×, 5× the intended clinical dose**, "with no clinically significant adverse events observed at these doses."
- STAY study as described **Jan 2026**: **1,300 dogs**, placebo-controlled, across the US, **fully enrolled**. (N grew from 1,000 → 1,300 between the two company statements — record the discrepancy.)
- Loyal, Jan 2026: "we are limited in how much detail we can provide about the testing process" until approval.
- Loyal's consumer site carries the repeated footnote **"*FDA approval not guaranteed."**

**Money:** Loyal has raised **>$150 million** to date (longevity.technology, 14 Jan 2026).

**The overstatement:** the milestone is *acceptance of one technical section of a conditional-approval application*. Headlines routinely render this as FDA "backing," "clearing," or "approving" a dog longevity drug. longevity.technology (14 Jan 2026) is one of the few to state it plainly: FDA "acceptance" is not approval; it means "the agency has reviewed Loyal's safety data and agrees that, under proposed conditions, the drug appears safe for use." If eventually approved, LOY-002 "would become the first FDA-approved drug intended to extend lifespan itself, in any species" — the conditional is doing all the work.

**Timeline slippage:** Feb 2025 Loyal anticipated "completed all the regulatory requirements by the end of 2025." As of Jan 2026, CMC still outstanding, and longevity.technology reports Loyal "expects to complete its final technical submission and apply for Expanded Conditional Approval next year." No peer-reviewed publication of the LOY-001/LOY-002 efficacy package located.

## 6. RAPAMYCIN OFF-LABEL AND THE CLINIC INDUSTRY

**Quantification of off-label use — weak.**
- The only cited peer-reviewed dataset: **Kaeberlein et al., "Evaluation of off-label rapamycin use to promote healthspan in 333 adults," GeroScience (2023), doi 10.1007/s11357-023-00818-1.** Survey of **333 adults with a history of off-label rapamycin use** plus **172 never-users**. Self-reported outcomes; ratios >3:1 in agreement for improvements in health, happiness, brain function, feelings of youthfulness, confidence, calmness, anxiety, generalized aches/pains; lower self-reported COVID/long-COVID. Authors' own caveat: cohort **was not blinded** and results "may have been impacted by some degree of placebo effect." **This is a self-selected, unblinded, self-reported survey — not evidence of effect.**
- A commercial page claims "more than 24 medical practices currently prescribe rapamycin as an anti-aging treatment." **No peer-reviewed census of US clinicians prescribing rapamycin for aging exists** as far as I could find. GAP.

**Price points (fetched from vendor pages, 2026-07-28):**
- **AgelessRx** (telehealth): rapamycin **"Starting at $65"** per month, "free shipping | free medical visit." Marketing copy on the page: **"Proven to extend the lifespan of every organism tested so far"** and **"Shown to improve healthspan and quality of life in aging adults."** Requires "willingness to take off-label Rapamycin," mandatory bloodwork, and termination for non-reporting. Page carries a DSHEA-style disclaimer — "The above statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease" — **on a page selling a prescription drug**, which is a category error worth flagging.

## 9. CROSS-CALIBER ISSUES
- **"FDA acceptance of a technical section" → "FDA approval."** Loyal has three technical sections (RXE, TAS, CMC) plus a post-conditional-approval pivotal study. Two are accepted. Press verbs used: "backs," "clears," "approves," "green light."
- **NAD market numbers mix incompatible scopes**: bulk NAD ingredient (food + pharma grade) vs finished NAD-precursor consumer supplements vs "longevity market." $488M (2021), $876M (2025), $1.42B, $3.9B (2025) are being quoted interchangeably. CAGRs from 6.1% to 18.2% for overlapping windows.
- **Longevity clinic count**: a survey of **82** self-selected clinics and a report-vendor claim of **">700 globally"** are cited in the same breath; neither is a census.
- **STAY study N**: 1,000 dogs (Loyal, Feb 2025) vs 1,300 dogs (Loyal, Jan 2026). Same trial, same company.
- **"Proven to reverse aging in dogs"**: numerator is a change in an owner-completed CCDR questionnaire score at 3 months (p=0.02); the objective in-house cognitive testing showed no between-group difference. Species: dog. Endpoint: subjective cognition. Not lifespan, not any aging measure.
- **AgelessRx: "Proven to extend the lifespan of every organism tested so far."** Rapamycin lifespan extension is *not* universal across tested organisms, and there is no human lifespan data at all. The sentence silently spans species and tiers.
- **Fountain Life's "46%" and "12%"** are presented as bare percentages with no N, denominator, comparator or time window.
- **The NARB holding is the general form of the whole problem**: "the required substantiation depends on the message conveyed to consumers, not the advertiser's regulatory classification" — i.e. a validated *biomarker* movement (NAD+ up) does not license a *functional benefit* claim.
