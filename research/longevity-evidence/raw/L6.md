## 1. THE REGULATORY FACT: "aging" is not an approved indication

### 1.1 Scoping review — no regulatory framework exists at all
Muscedere J, Shorey CL, Duque G, Kim P, Lorbergs AL, McGlory C, Merchant RA, Newman JC, Rolland Y, Vellas B.
"Advancing geroscience research – A scoping review of regulatory environments for gerotherapeutics."
*J Nutr Health Aging* 2025;29(9):100637. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12310402/
- Screened **3,780 publications**.
- VERBATIM: "Regulatory bodies such as the FDA, the EMA and Health Canada do not classify aging as a disease, which complicates the approval process for therapies targeting aging."
- Found **no** established regulatory frameworks for gerotherapeutics.
- Funding: Canadian Frailty Network. COI: "The authors declare that they have no known competing financial interests..."
- CAUTION / CROSS-CALIBER: this same paper states "Only one study—the Targeting Aging with Metformin (TAME) trial—has been approved by the FDA to investigate an intervention targeting multiple age-related diseases rather than a single condition." This wording ("approved by the FDA") is the standard field overstatement — see §1.3. FDA does not "approve" trials; it clears INDs and gives non-binding advice at pre-IND/EOP2 meetings.

### 1.2 What FDA itself says (NIA relay page)
NIA, "Information on FDA review of geroscience-related IND applications," nia.nih.gov.
(Direct WebFetch returned HTTP 405; content captured via search index — flag as needing re-verification.)
- FDA will consider accelerated approval "based on evidence that the proposed surrogate or intermediate clinical endpoint is reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit."
- FDA recognizes biomarkers as *drug development tools* (risk/prognosis, target engagement, safety) and has a formal biomarker qualification process — but no aging biomarker has gone through it (see gaps).
- Nothing here creates an "aging" indication.

### 1.3 TAME: what was actually agreed, and the trial's real status
- Barzilai N, Crandall JP, Kritchevsky SB, Espeland MA. "Metformin as a tool to target aging." *Cell Metab* 2016;23(6):1060–1065. PMID 27304507.
  - Abstract VERBATIM: "Aging has been targeted by genetic and dietary manipulation and by drugs in order to increase lifespan and health span in numerous models. Metformin, which has demonstrated protective effects against several age-related diseases in humans, will be tested in the TAME (Targeting Aging with Metformin) trial, as the initial step in the development of increasingly effective next-generation drugs."
  - **The abstract of the field's foundational TAME paper contains no mention of the FDA.** The "FDA agreed to the composite endpoint" claim is sourced overwhelmingly to secondary/advocacy material (AFAR fundraising pages, podcasts, trade press), not to a primary FDA document. I could not locate any FDA letter, guidance, or Federal Register notice accepting a composite morbidity endpoint for an aging indication.
- AFAR TAME page (afar.org/tame-trial), fetched 2026-07-28. VERBATIM: "We hope the FDA will approve aging as an indication, to signify that aging can be 'treated.'"
  - Design: "3,000 individuals between the ages of 65-79"; "Six-year clinical trials."
  - Status section still lists participants and "Visionary donors to help launch the multi-year, multi-site TAME Trial" under WHAT'S NEEDED. Study design "has been expertly prepared."
  - i.e. **as of 2026-07-28 AFAR's own page describes TAME in prospective, unfunded, unlaunched terms.**
- **ClinicalTrials.gov: TAME is not registered.** API query `query.term="Targeting Aging with Metformin"` → totalCount = 1, and the single hit is NCT05651620 "The CALERIE™ Legacy Study" (Tufts, n=216, started 2023-04-06), i.e. a false positive. A second query `query.term=TAME metformin aging` → same single hit. No Albert Einstein / Barzilai TAME record exists.
  - This is the single hardest checkable fact on this line: a trial announced in 2015–2016, universally described in the press as "FDA-approved" and "underway," has **no registry entry eleven years later**.
- Cross-caliber warning: search engines surfaced pages (imedic.health "Metformin Shows Anti-Aging Benefits ... TAME trial results 2026"; geroevidence.com; longevity.direct; mullosaint.com; research.mental-momentum.ai) asserting TAME "enrolled ~3,000 participants" and will report in 2028–2029. These are content-farm/AI-generated pages with no primary source. Record as hype, not evidence.

### 1.4 The Loyal dog precedent (endpoint angle only; L7 owns commerce)
- Loyal, "Not just for big dogs! Our senior dog program receives preliminary FDA efficacy acceptance (RXE)," loyal.com, announced **2025-02-26**.
  - VERBATIM: "LOY-002, our program bringing longevity to dogs of almost all sizes, has also earned its formal FDA acceptance for reasonable expectation of efficacy (RXE)."
  - Target population: dogs ≥10 years old, ≥14 lb. Stated aim: extend healthy lifespan by mitigating age-associated metabolic dysfunction.
  - Explicit caveat VERBATIM: "The other two primary FDA requirements needed for conditional approval (and our market launch) are manufacturing and safety."
  - The company did not publish the STAY study N, duration, or primary endpoint on that page.
- Follow-on: Businesswire 2026-01-13, "Loyal Receives FDA Acceptance of Safety Package for Senior Dog Lifespan Extension Drug" — TAS section accepted; manufacturing section outstanding. (Fetch timed out; headline verified via two independent indexes incl. dvm360.)
- ENDPOINT SIGNIFICANCE: this is FDA **Center for Veterinary Medicine**, on the **conditional approval** pathway (Expanded Conditional Approval, animal drugs), where "reasonable expectation of effectiveness" is an explicitly *lower* evidentiary bar than substantial evidence of effectiveness, granted *before* the confirmatory effectiveness study is complete. It is a precedent that a regulator will entertain "extension of healthy lifespan" as an indication wording — in dogs. It is **not** a human-drug precedent, not a CDER action, and not a demonstration that the drug works.

### 1.5 Nosology sidebar: WHO ICD-11
- WHO's proposed ICD-11 code MG2A "old age" was withdrawn after formal consultation and replaced, effective ~2022-07-08, by "Ageing associated decline in intrinsic capacity" under General Symptoms.
- Primary-ish source: Lancet Healthy Longevity 2022, "How 'old age' was withdrawn as a diagnosis from ICD-11" (thelancet.com PIIS2666-7568(22)00102-7). **Fetch returned HTTP 403 — quote not verified verbatim; treat as second-hand until re-checked.**
- Advocacy sites (longevityforall.org, longevityalliance.org) spin this as "aging is a medical target in ICD-11 with 2 codes." That is the hype reading; the actual decision moved *away* from coding chronological age as a diagnosis.

## 2. EPIGENETIC CLOCKS AS SURROGATE ENDPOINTS

### 2.1 Generations
- Gen 1 (trained on chronological age): Horvath 2013 multi-tissue clock; Hannum 2013. Horvath skin-and-blood clock 2018.
- Gen 2 (trained on mortality/phenotype): PhenoAge (Levine 2018), GrimAge (Lu 2019), GrimAge2.
- Gen 3 (pace of change): DunedinPoAm 2020, DunedinPACE 2022.
- Gen 4 (causality-enriched): CausAge, DamAge, AdaptAge (Ying et al. 2024).

### 2.2 Reliability — the load-bearing problem
**Higgins-Chen AT, Thrush KL, Wang Y, et al. (Levine ME senior). "A computational solution for bolstering reliability of epigenetic clocks: implications for clinical trials and longitudinal tracking." *Nature Aging* 2022;2:644–661. PMID 36277076.**
- Abstract VERBATIM: "Epigenetic clocks are widely used aging biomarkers calculated from DNA methylation data, but this data can be surprisingly unreliable. Here we show technical noise produces deviations up to 9 years between replicates for six prominent epigenetic clocks, limiting their utility. We present a computational solution to bolster reliability, calculating principal components from CpG-level data as input for biological age prediction. Our retrained principal-component versions of six clocks show agreement between most replicates within 1.5 years..."
- Caliber: **technical replicates** — the *same DNA sample* run twice. Not biological variation. Not between-person. Deviations up to **9 years** on the same sample.
- Independent validation set: "Reliability of epigenetic age and age acceleration in an independent blood DNAm dataset with **37 pairs of technical replicates** (Elysium Dataset 1)" (Extended Data Fig. 4b). Main reliability analyses used GSE55763 (Lehne), 36 replicate pairs.
- **COI (VERBATIM, in full):** "M.E.L. and A.T.H.-C. have built epigenetic aging metrics involving the technology described in the present paper, and these metrics are licensed by Elysium Health through Yale University. Elysium provided paired blood and saliva replicate datasets reported in this study, but otherwise did not fund the study and did not play a role in conceptualization, design, decision to publish or preparation of the manuscript. M.E.L. previously acted as a Scientific Advisor for, and received consulting fees from, Elysium Health. T.H.S. was previously an employee of Elysium Health. A.T.H.-C. received consulting fees from FOXO Technologies for work unrelated to the present manuscript. All other authors declare no competing interests."
  - i.e. the paper that documents first-generation clock unreliability, and sells the fix, is authored by people whose fix is licensed to a company that sells a consumer biological-age test, using that company's data.
- NOT OBTAINED: the per-clock ICC table (Horvath1 / Horvath2 / Hannum / PhenoAge / GrimAge / DNAmTL original vs PC). Nature full text is paywalled past the abstract+figure captions; PMC (PMC9586209) is behind a reCAPTCHA; bioRxiv preprint 403s. **Do not cite per-clock ICC numbers from memory or from search snippets** — several snippets circulated contradictory values (e.g. "GrimAge ICC > 0.9" vs "no clock reached excellent"). Flagged in crossCaliberIssues.

**Belsky DW, Caspi A, Corcoran DL, et al. "DunedinPACE, a DNA methylation biomarker of the pace of aging." *eLife* 2022;11:e73420.**
- VERBATIM: "For DunedinPACE, reliability was excellent (ICC = 0.96 [0.93–0.98]" — GSE55763, n=36 replicate pairs.
- Also: EPIC-EPIC replicate database n=28 → "DunedinPACE ICCs were 0.97 [0.94–0.98]"; **450k-to-EPIC cross-platform** database n=350 → "0.87 [0.82–0.90]".
- VERBATIM (original DunedinPoAm): "For the original DunedinPoAm, reliability was good (ICC = 0.89 95% CI [0.79–0.94]".
- Caliber note that matters: DunedinPACE achieved this partly by **pre-selecting CpG probes for high test-retest reliability**. That is reliability by construction; it does not establish that the construct tracks anything causal. Also note the drop from 0.96–0.97 (same platform) to 0.87 (across array versions) — platform change alone degrades it.
- eLife page did not display a competing-interests block in the fetched render; Duke/Belsky hold IP on the Pace-of-Aging measures and TruDiagnostic licenses DunedinPACE commercially — **NOT verified from a primary document in this session; must be checked before publication.**

**Apsley AT, Ye Q, Caspi A, ... Sugden K, Shalev I. "Cross-tissue comparison of epigenetic aging clocks in humans." *Aging Cell* 2025;24(4):e14451.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11984668/
- Design: 83 individuals (47 adults 19–70 y; 36 children 9–14 y), 284 tissue samples: buccal n=81, saliva n=59, dried blood spots n=64, buffy coat n=35 (children), PBMC n=45 (adults).
- Within-person, same-person differences between tissues:
  - GrimAge2: buccal vs DBS **35.78 ± 0.68 years**; buccal vs PBMC **40.25 ± 0.79 years**.
  - Hannum: buccal vs DBS **26.18 ± 0.78 years**.
  - PhenoAge: buccal vs DBS **20.96 ± 1.00 years**.
- DBS vs buffy coat (both blood): "no significant differences in all clocks tested." Horvath skin-and-blood clock most concordant across tissues.
- Conclusion VERBATIM: "Tissue type plays an important role in the estimation of biological age and should be carefully considered when using epigenetic clocks."
- Funding NIH (U01ES030949, P50HD089922, UL1TR002014, UL1TR000045, U24AG066528) + Penn State. COI: "None declared."
- **This is the closest thing to a head-to-head DTC vendor comparison in the peer-reviewed literature**: the consumer market is split between blood-based (TruDiagnostic) and saliva/cheek-based (Elysium Index) sampling, and this paper shows the same person can be assigned biological ages ~30–40 years apart depending purely on which tissue the vendor swabs.

**Bell CG, Lowe R, Adams PD, et al. "DNA methylation aging clocks: challenges and recommendations." *Genome Biology* 2019;20:249.**
- VERBATIM: "there is not one measure or 'gold standard' of biological aging."
- VERBATIM: clocks "can predict all-cause mortality at a population, but not individual level."
- VERBATIM: elastic-net-derived clocks give "limited mechanistic insight due to their black box nature."
- Note: arrays assay "only ~3% of all the CpG sites in the human genome."
- The paper gives no endorsement of clinical-trial or DTC use.

### 2.3 Has ANY clock been validated as a surrogate endpoint (regulatory sense)? — NO. Verbatim sources:

**Cummings SR, Kritchevsky SB. "Endpoints for geroscience clinical trials: health outcomes, biomarkers, and biologic age." *GeroScience* 2022;44(6):2925–2931. PMID 36260264.**
- Abstract VERBATIM: "Using biomarkers instead of clinical outcomes would substantially reduce the size and the duration of clinical trials. This requires validation of surrogate markers showing that treatment induced change in the marker reliably predicts the magnitude of change in the clinical outcome. The surrogate marker must also reflect the biological mechanism for the effect of treatment on the clinical outcome. 'Biological age' is a superficially attractive marker for such trials. However, it is essential to establish that treatment induced change in biological age reliably predict the magnitude of benefits in the clinical outcome."
- This is the exact criterion the field has not met for any clock: change-in-marker → change-in-outcome.

**Rolland Y, Sierra F, Ferrucci L, et al. "Challenges in developing Geroscience trials." *Nature Communications* 2023;14:5038.**
- VERBATIM: "Currently there are no consensually accepted biomarkers of aging."
- VERBATIM: "Mortality and longevity are outcomes that are difficult to apply in randomized controlled trials because they require long-term follow-up and large sample sizes. A study with the sole objective of lifespan extension would take decades and would be very expensive."
- VERBATIM (on TAME, note the conditional): "The TAME trial may serve as a proof of concept that proves to the medical agencies that aging can be a therapeutic indication in itself." — i.e. even sympathetic insiders phrase the regulatory status as *hoped-for*, not achieved.
- Describes TAME as 3,000 subjects aged 65–80 across 14 US institutes, time-to-first of five major age-related conditions over six years.

**Moqri M, Herzog C, Poganik JR, Ying K, Justice JN, Belsky DW, ... Ferrucci L. "Validation of biomarkers of aging." *Nature Medicine* 2024;30(2):360–372. PMID 38355974.**
- Abstract VERBATIM: "Such biomarkers could predict aging-related outcomes and could serve as surrogate endpoints for the evaluation of interventions promoting healthy aging and longevity. However, **no consensus exists on how biomarkers of aging should be validated before their translation to the clinic.**"
- Note the tense: *could* serve. The consortium that most wants clocks to be surrogate endpoints says the validation methodology itself does not yet exist.

### 2.4 Biomarkers of Aging Consortium — what they concede

**Biomarkers of Aging Consortium; Herzog CMS, Goeminne LJE, Poganik JR, Barzilai N, Belsky DW, ... Moqri M, Gladyshev VN. "Challenges and recommendations for the translation of biomarkers of aging." *Nature Aging* 2024;4:1372–1383 (publ. 2024-09-16). doi:10.1038/s43587-024-00683-3**
- Method caliber: expert questionnaire, **n = 29 respondents of 34 invited**, drawn from the 2023 Biomarkers of Aging Symposium; authors state the pool "predominantly comprised preclinical academic scientists."
- **THE CIRCULARITY ADMISSION (Table 1, "Arguments against" the criterion 'Responds to putative geroprotectors'), VERBATIM:** "No existing geroprotectors have been validated in humans. This may lead to a circularity argument, in which biomarkers are developed using changes induced by putative interventions and thus 'validate' these interventions but do not necessarily truly capture a geroprotective effect."
- **THE FAILED-SURROGATE PRECEDENT, VERBATIM:** "some very good predictors may still be poor surrogate endpoints, in particular when predictive and response properties have different mechanisms. For instance, increases in lean body and appendicular mass (assumed surrogate endpoints for functional measures) were not associated with improvements in leg press strength, gait speed or other health endpoints in a randomized controlled trial of a sarcopenia treatment; these and other examples of failed surrogate endpoints underscore the need to ultimately understand causal mechanisms."
- VERBATIM on regulatory need: "Such evidence will be critical for regulatory approval (for example, by the Food and Drug Administration and the European Medicines Agency)..." — framed as future work.
- VERBATIM on current state: "Application of BOA for both clinical research and routine implementation (including decision making) remains minimal."
- Table 2 challenge (4): "There is no clear consensus on the minimal criteria for clinical implementation of a biomarker of aging."
- Table 2 challenge (3): "No consensus exists on whether BOA may be applied throughout lifespan or only later in life."
- Roughly 40% of these experts said they personally track their own health with a biomarker of aging; those who don't cited "a caution over individual predictiveness ('we are not there yet')."

**Ying K, Paulson S, Eames A, ... Poganik JR, Moqri M, Gladyshev VN. "A unified framework for systematic curation and evaluation of aging biomarkers." *Nature Aging* 2025 (publ. 2025-11-04). doi:10.1038/s43587-025-00987-y**
- Abstract VERBATIM: "Here we show that the ability of traditional aging clocks to predict chronological age does not correlate with mortality prediction capacity (**R = 0.12, P = 0.67**), suggesting that these metrics capture distinct biological processes."
- Scale: "standardized evaluation of **39 biomarkers across over 20,000 individuals** from diverse cohorts."
- Head-to-head: "The Horvath skin and blood clock achieved the highest chronological age accuracy (**R² = 0.88**), while GrimAge2 demonstrated the strongest mortality association (**hazard ratio = 2.57**) and healthspan prediction (**hazard ratio = 2.00**)."
- VERBATIM: "Our systematic evaluation reveals considerable heterogeneity in biomarker performance across different clinical outcomes, with optimal biomarkers varying according to specific application."
- Caliber note: HRs are observational associations per unit of the biomarker in cohorts, not intervention effects. Nothing here is a surrogate validation.

**Cipriano A, Justice J, Poganik JR, Moqri M, ... Furman D, Herzog CMS. "Immune aging biomarkers for clinical trials." *Nature Medicine* 2026;32:2368–2382 (publ. 2026-07-03).**
- Abstract VERBATIM: "Despite this central role, **there is no consensus on how to quantify immune aging, especially in clinical trials.**"
- Dated three weeks before this research run — the "no consensus" position is current, not historical.

## 4. WHY A HUMAN LIFESPAN RCT IS EFFECTIVELY IMPOSSIBLE

- **Published statement (weak form, but it is the real published state of the art):** Rolland et al., *Nat Commun* 2023: "Mortality and longevity are outcomes that are difficult to apply in randomized controlled trials because they require long-term follow-up and large sample sizes. A study with the sole objective of lifespan extension would take decades and would be very expensive."
- Cummings & Kritchevsky 2022 make the same structural point from the other side: biomarkers would "substantially reduce the size and the duration of clinical trials" — the admission that the outcome trial is otherwise unaffordable.
- **HONEST GAP:** I could not find a peer-reviewed paper that publishes an explicit power calculation for a human all-cause-mortality / lifespan RCT of a geroprotector (required N, years of follow-up, dollar cost). The field asserts infeasibility qualitatively and moves on. Even TAME — the field's flagship — is powered on a *composite morbidity* endpoint over 6 years in n≈3,000, not on lifespan; and TAME itself has never been registered or funded. **The strongest honest statement is: nobody has published the number, which is itself the finding.**
- Bounding intuition available from TAME's own design (3,000 × 6 years for a composite of five morbidity outcomes + death) — a pure-mortality version in a healthier/younger cohort would need to be far larger and far longer. Do not present any specific N/cost figure as sourced; none is.

## 5. HALLMARKS OF AGING FRAMEWORK

- López-Otín C, Blasco MA, Partridge L, Serrano M, Kroemer G. "The Hallmarks of Aging." *Cell* 2013;153(6):1194–1217. Nine hallmarks.
- López-Otín C, Blasco MA, Partridge L, Serrano M, Kroemer G. "Hallmarks of aging: An expanding universe." *Cell* 2023;186(2):243–278. Twelve hallmarks (adds disabled macroautophagy, chronic inflammation, dysbiosis).
  - The expansion from 9→12 in ten years is itself the methodological point: a framework that grows by accretion when new candidates appear is a taxonomy, not a theory that could have been refuted.
- **CRITIQUE — Gems D, de Magalhães JP. "The hoverfly and the wasp: A critique of the hallmarks of aging as a paradigm." *Ageing Research Reviews* 2021;70:101407.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7611451/
  - VERBATIM: "while the hallmarks of cancer provide a paradigmatic account of the causes of cancer with profound explanatory power, the hallmarks of aging do not."
  - VERBATIM: "The hallmarks of aging, as a pseudo-paradigm, risks acting as a conceptual obstacle to the development of a genuine paradigm with real explanatory power."
  - VERBATIM: "The aging hallmarks account does not really provide an explanatory framework in terms of primary causes of aging."
  - VERBATIM: the nine topics "are not hallmarks in the Hanahan and Weinberg sense" and the framework "dresses them up as a paradigm, even though a genuine paradigm as present in the hallmarks of cancer account does not exist."
  - Title metaphor: the hoverfly mimics the wasp's warning coloration without the sting — the hallmarks paper looks like the hallmarks-of-cancer paradigm without its explanatory machinery.
- Later philosophical critique: "The hallmarks of aging: Paradigms and scientific progress," *Ageing Research Reviews* 2026 (S156816372600173X) — reports unresolved ontological and causal issues and insufficient engagement with competing paradigms. **Not fetched; verify before citing.**
- Why this belongs in L6: if the underlying framework is a list rather than a falsifiable theory, then "targets a hallmark of aging" carries no predictive weight about clinical benefit — it is a category membership claim, not evidence. Most tier-1→tier-3 promotion in this field routes through that move.

## 6. TIER DISCIPLINE (the promotion mechanism this line exists to name)

- Tier 1 — animal lifespan/healthspan (mouse, worm, fly, dog).
- Tier 2 — human surrogate endpoint (epigenetic clock, inflammatory marker, grip strength proxy). Everything in §2 lives here, and §2.3 shows nothing in this tier is validated as predicting tier-3 change.
- Tier 3 — human clinical outcome (disease incidence, disability, death). ClinicalTrials.gov: 15 of 3,076 interventional aging trials have this as a primary outcome.
- The silent promotion is: tier-1 mouse result + tier-2 clock movement, reported as if tier-3. The Consortium's own circularity quote (§2.4) is the field admitting the loop is closed on itself.

## 8. CROSS-CALIBER ISSUES / HYPE LEDGER

- "The FDA approved the TAME trial / approved aging as an indication" — false in the strong reading, unsourced in the weak reading. Even Muscedere 2025 (peer-reviewed) uses "approved by the FDA," which is the wrong verb for what happens at a pre-IND meeting.
- "9 years" (Higgins-Chen) is a **technical-replicate** deviation on the same DNA — it is often quoted as if it were biological variation or measurement error between visits. Different caliber entirely.
- DunedinPACE ICC 0.96 is same-platform; the same paper reports 0.87 across array generations (n=350). Vendors quote the 0.96.
- GrimAge2 HR 2.57 for mortality is an observational hazard ratio in cohorts; it is routinely deployed as if it licensed "lower your GrimAge, lower your mortality."
- Search results contained multiple AI-generated content-farm pages asserting TAME results, 2026 metformin "anti-aging benefits," and vendor accuracy claims with no primary source. Treat any TAME "results" claim as fabricated until a registry entry exists.
- Reliability snippets in search indexes gave mutually contradictory ICCs ("GrimAge ICC > 0.9" vs "no clock reached excellent, ICC 0.4–0.7"). Do not use any per-clock ICC not read from the primary table.
