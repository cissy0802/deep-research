Working notes. Compiled 2026-07-28. Every number below was pulled from the primary paper
(PMC / PubMed / publisher abstract) unless explicitly flagged as secondary.

## 1. What the ITP is, and why the design is the animal-evidence gold standard

Primary design papers:
- Miller RA et al. "An aging Interventions Testing Program: study design and interim report."
  Aging Cell 2007;6(4):565-75. doi:10.1111/j.1474-9726.2007.00311.x
- Nadon NL, Strong R, Miller RA, Nelson J, Javors M, Sharp ZD, Peralba JM, Harrison DE.
  "Design of aging intervention studies: the NIA interventions testing program."
  Age (Dordr) 2008;30(4):187-99. doi:10.1007/s11357-008-9048-1. PMC2585647
- Nadon NL, Strong R, Miller RA, Harrison DE. "NIA Interventions Testing Program: Investigating
  Putative Aging Intervention Agents in a Genetically Heterogeneous Mouse Model."
  eBioMedicine 2017;21:3-4. PMC5514387

### Verbatim design facts (Nadon 2008, PMC2585647)

- Cross: "The specific stock of HET mice used in the ITP is a cross of four inbred strains,
  generated by breeding two F1 hybrids, CB6F1 X C3D2F1."
  (i.e. BALB/cByJ x C57BL/6J dams crossed to C3H/HeJ x DBA/2J sires — every mouse is a
  genetically unique full sib, none inbred. Later ITP papers write it "CByB6F1 × C3D2F1".)
- N: "Each experimental group includes 36 females and 44 males per site, for a total of
  108 females and 132 males for each intervention tested."
- Power: "A power analysis was performed...to determine the number of mice needed per
  experimental group to be able to detect a 10% change in lifespan as compared to control
  groups, at 80% power."
- Selection: "The ITP solicits proposals from the extramural research community for compounds,
  supplements and diets that have potential to extend lifespan and promote healthy aging.
  The proposals go through a two-tier review and prioritization process, using experts from
  the research community to evaluate the scientific merit, feasibility and priority."
- Husbandry: "The ITP developed SOPs to ensure that environmental conditions at the three sites
  are as uniform as possible. Lighting, temperature, housing, and diet are controlled and
  monitored." / "The ITP mice are fed NIH31 diet with 4% fat, available ad libitum...The food is
  prepared and sterilized, and then the test compounds are added."
- Start age: "Most treatment protocols begin feeding the experimental diets at 4 months of age,
  although some have been started later in life."
- Stats: "The primary statistical method was to use the log-rank test with stratification by
  site, so that each of the five treatment groups (four experimental diets plus the control)
  was compared to the pooled survival experience at each test site."

### Verbatim (Nadon 2017 eBioMedicine, PMC5514387)

- "Lifespan tests are done in parallel, using the same protocol, at three independent sites to
  increase robustness of the findings."
- "Population sizes are large enough that the protocol will detect a 10% change in mean lifespan,
  in either sex, with 80% power, pooling data from as few as two sites."
- "The ITP uses a four-way cross genetically heterogeneous mouse model (UM-HET3) to reduce the
  impact of strain-specific characteristics on outcomes."
- ITP's own limitation, verbatim: "the ITP has consistently found that control male mice at one
  site weigh less and live longer than the control males at the other two sites, even though
  each site uses the same food preparations and standardized husbandry." And:
  "site-to-site variation is unavoidable even with every effort made to minimize differences
  between sites."

### Verbatim (Nadon/Miller "ITP: an update", PMC6241422)

- "Interventions proposed by scientists from the research community are initially tested, in
  parallel, at three sites (Jackson Laboratories, University of Michigan and the University of
  Texas Health Science Center at San Antonio) in male and female genetically heterogeneous
  UM-HET3 mice using identical, standardized protocols."
- "The use of genetically heterogeneous mice greatly reduces the possibility that the results are
  only valid for a single strain."
- "Fifty-three lifespan experiments, involving 30 test agents, were initiated in the first 11
  years of the ITP. Significant effects on longevity, in one or both sexes, have been published
  for 6 of the tested agents." [as of ~2015]

### NOT FOUND / caveat on "publishes regardless of outcome"
I could NOT find an explicit ITP policy sentence saying "we publish all results regardless of
outcome" in the design papers I could reach (Nadon 2008, Nadon 2017, Miller update). The claim is
strongly supported by *behaviour* — see the paper titles: "...do not significantly affect lifespan
in either sex" (GeroScience 2024), "...do not increase lifespan in UM-HET3 mice" (GeroScience 2026),
"Rapamycin, but NOT resveratrol or simvastatin..." (J Gerontol 2011). Treat "publishes negatives"
as an inference from the publication record, not a quoted policy.

### Control lifespan baseline (needed for magnitude framing)
Cheng CJ, Gelfond JAL, Strong R, Nelson JF. "Genetically heterogeneous mice exhibit a female
survival advantage that is age- and site-specific: Results from a large multi-site study."
Aging Cell 2019;18(3):e12905. PMC6516160
- "Analysis of age-specific survival of 3,690 control ITP mice" across "six separate studies
  conducted between the years of 2004 and 2013."
- Control females: median 887 days (95% CI 879-898). Control males: median 803 days (95% CI 791-815).
- "The mortality difference is greatest in the first half of life, peaking around 350 days of age
  and decreasing steadily thereafter until reaching convergence around the median lifespan."
- Site heterogeneity: "no difference in median lifespan or overall survival between males and
  females at UM" in the pooled dataset, while UT and TJL showed a persistent sex difference.
  => Site effects are real and large enough to flip a sex difference. This is the ITP's own data.

## 2. The compound ledger

Master index: Mouse Phenome Database ITP data portal, https://phenome.jax.org/projects/ITP1
(useful navigational index; ALWAYS confirm numbers against the paper — MPD lists p-values and
doses per cohort but the published papers are the citable caliber).

### RAPAMYCIN — the largest and most replicated hit

**(a) Harrison DE et al. Nature 2009;460(7253):392-5. doi:10.1038/nature08221. PMC2786175**
Abstract, verbatim: "we report that rapamycin, an inhibitor of the mTOR pathway, extends median
and maximal lifespan of both male and female mice when fed beginning at 600 days of age. On the
basis of age at 90% mortality, rapamycin led to an increase of 14% for females and 9% for males."
- CALIBER TRAP: the famous "14% / 9%" is **age at 90% mortality** (a maximum-lifespan measure),
  NOT median. Median increases in the same 600-day cohort were ~13% (F) and ~9% (M) pooled.
  Journalists routinely reprint 14% as "median lifespan".
- Dose 14 ppm encapsulated rapamycin (eudragit-encapsulated, LC Labs) in Purina 5LG6 chow.
- Log-rank pooled p<0.0001 both sexes; site-specific p 0.03-0.0001. ~1901 mice at analysis.
- Authors' own hedge, verbatim: "Rapamycin may extend lifespan by postponing death from cancer,
  by retarding mechanisms of ageing, or both." And: the median+maximum increase is "consistent
  with the hypothesis that inhibiting the mTORC1 pathway retards mammalian ageing, but is not
  compelling proof."
- Backstory: an earlier attempt to start at 270 days failed on formulation (rapamycin degraded
  in chow); the 600-day start was a rescue, which is WHY the famous result is a late-life start.
  The 270-day cohort was reported later (Miller 2011).

**(b) Miller RA et al. J Gerontol A 2011;66(2):191-201. doi:10.1093/gerona/glq178. PMC3021372**
"Rapamycin, but not resveratrol or simvastatin, extends life span of genetically heterogeneous mice."
- Rapamycin 14 ppm from 9 months: median survival extended "an average of 10% in males and
  18% in females"; significant at each of three sites; maximum lifespan also increased.
- Resveratrol 300 and 1200 ppm: no significant effect. Simvastatin 12 and 120 ppm: no effect.

**(c) Miller RA et al. Aging Cell 2014;13(3):468-77. doi:10.1111/acel.12194. PMC4032600**
"Rapamycin-mediated lifespan increase in mice is dose and sex dependent and metabolically
distinct from dietary restriction." Start age 9 months.
| Dose | Male median | Female median |
|---|---|---|
| 4.7 ppm | +3% (p=0.19, NS) | +16% (p<0.0001) |
| 14 ppm | +13% (p=0.0015) | +21% (p<0.0001) |
| 42 ppm | +23% (p<0.0001) | +26% (p<0.0001) |
- 90th percentile at 42 ppm: +8% males (p=0.001), +11% females (p<0.001).
- Blood levels after 5 months: F 7/16/80 ng/mL vs M 6/9/23 ng/mL at 4.7/14/42 ppm.
  Sex p=0.007, rapamycin p=0.003, interaction p=0.03.
- Verbatim: "Rapamycin leads to a larger percentage increase in female longevity than in males,
  even though female controls live longer than male controls at TJL and UT. It seems likely that
  this differential effect, seen now in three consecutive independent cohorts, reflects higher
  levels of rapamycin in blood of female UM-HET3 mice compared with males."
- NOTE: at the LOW dose (4.7 ppm) rapamycin did essentially nothing in males (+3%, NS).
  Rapamycin is NOT a male-biased drug — it is the main FEMALE-favouring hit in the ITP.

**(d) Strong R et al. Aging Cell 2020;19(11):e13269. doi:10.1111/acel.13269. PMC7681050**
Late-life regimens, all 42 ppm, all starting at 20 months:
| Regimen | Male median | Female median |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous from 20 mo | +11% (p=0.0007) | +15% (p<0.0001) |
| Alternating month on/off | +9% (p=0.002) | +8% (p<0.0001) |
| 3-month pulse (20-23 mo only) | +11% (p=0.024) | +4% (p=0.15, NS) |
Also tested and null in both sexes: 17-DMAG 30 ppm from 6 mo; minocycline 300 ppm from 6 mo;
beta-guanidinopropionic acid 3300 ppm from 6 mo; MitoQ 100 ppm from 7 mo.
Authors: "developing clinical strategies for optimal benefit in long-term Rapa treatment...may
require careful stepwise refinement and adjustment for sex-specific effects."

### METFORMIN — the single most important null in the whole field

**Strong R et al. Aging Cell 2016;15(5):872-84. doi:10.1111/acel.12496. PMC5013015**
- Metformin alone, 0.1% of diet (1000 ppm), started 9 months, both sexes, 3 sites.
- Verbatim: "Met alone, at a dose of 0.1% in the diet, did not significantly extend lifespan."
- Males: "7% increase in median lifespan of males when data were pooled across sites, but the
  effect was not statistically significant (P = 0.35)". Females: "There was no effect of Met on
  survival of female mice."
- CROSS-CALIBER WARNING: the 2024 Gehan reanalysis lists the SAME metformin experiment as
  "+8% median, log-rank p = 0.2200". The 2016 paper says +7%, p=0.35. Different analysis
  (site-stratified pooling vs. plain pooled log-rank, and possibly slightly different censoring
  date). Either way: NOT significant by the pre-specified log-rank test. Do not quote a single
  p-value without saying which analysis.
- Metformin + rapamycin combination (Met 1000 ppm + Rapa 14 ppm, from 9 months): "23% increase
  in survival compared with controls in both sexes", P<0.001. The ITP's own read is that this is
  not clearly better than rapamycin 14 ppm alone in females (14 ppm alone gave +21% F / +13% M
  in Miller 2014); the male number is where the combination looks additive. That comparison is
  ACROSS COHORTS, not concurrent — treat as hypothesis, not result.
- Only ITP metformin test found: one dose, one start age. No higher dose, no later start,
  no metformin-in-a-different-vehicle test has been published. => GAP.

**Jiang N, Gelfond J, Liu Q, Strong R, Nelson JF. GeroScience 2024;46(5):4533-41.
doi:10.1007/s11357-024-01161-9. PMC11335987**
"The Gehan test identifies life-extending compounds overlooked by the log-rank test in the NIA
Interventions Testing Program..."
Abstract verbatim: "The National Institute on Aging Interventions Testing Program (ITP) has so
far identified 12 compounds that extend the lifespan of genetically heterogeneous mice using the
log-rank test. However, the log-rank test is relatively insensitive to any compound that does not
uniformly reduce mortality across the lifespan... We therefore reanalyzed all data collected by
the ITP from 2004-2022 using the Gehan test... The Gehan test identified 5 additional compounds,
metformin, enalapril, 17-dimethylaminoethylamino-17-demethoxygeldanamycin hydrochloride (17-DMAG),
caffeic acid phenethyl ester (CAPE), and green tea extract (GTE), which significantly increased
survival but were previously missed by the log-rank test. Three (metformin, enalapril, and 17-DMAG)
were only effective in males and two (CAPE and GTE) were only effective in females."
- Table 1 (as transcribed): metformin m, 1000 ppm, onset 9 mo, +8% median, log-rank p=0.2200,
  Gehan p=0.0460, Z-test p=0.0343, PH assumption VIOLATED.
  (Results text elsewhere in the paper quotes p=0.0495 for metformin males — my two extraction
  passes disagreed on which p attaches to metformin vs. GTE-male. FLAG FOR VERIFICATION against
  the PDF table before printing a specific Gehan p-value.)
- Authors' own caveat, verbatim: "We recognize that secondary analyses increase the risk of
  type 1 error (i.e. increased false positives)."
- Authors are UT Health San Antonio (Nelson, Strong, Gelfond) — i.e. ITP-affiliated insiders,
  NOT an independent adversarial reanalysis. Funding 5U01AG022307 (an ITP grant), 5P30AG013319,
  VA IK6 BX006289. "The authors declare no competing interests."
- How to use this honestly: this is a post-hoc change of statistical test, by the same group,
  on the same data, applied to 132 comparisons, that promotes metformin from null to marginal.
  It is a reason to say "metformin's mouse null is not airtight", not a reason to say
  "metformin extends mouse lifespan".

### ACARBOSE — the biggest male effect

**Harrison DE et al. Aging Cell 2014;13(2):273-82. doi:10.1111/acel.12170. PMC3954939**
- ACA 1000 ppm from 4 months. Male median +22% (P<0.0001); female median +5% (P=0.01).
- 90th percentile: +11% males (P<0.001), +9% females (P=0.001).
- 17-alpha-estradiol 4.8 ppm from 10 months: male median +12% (P=0.002); "did not alter female
  lifespan"; no effect on male maximum lifespan.
- NDGA 800 / 2500 / 5000 ppm: male median +8-10%, P 0.04 to 0.005; no female benefit.
- Authors on the sex asymmetry (NDGA): "the lack of effect in females is not due simply to
  altered pharmacodynamics" despite similar blood levels; hypothesis that NDGA "produces
  beneficial effects in both sexes but produces harmful effects in females that prevent
  lifespan extension."

**Harrison DE et al. Aging Cell 2019;18(2):e12898. doi:10.1111/acel.12898 — FULL ABSTRACT VERBATIM:**
"To follow-up on our previous report that acarbose (ACA), a drug that blocks postprandial glucose
spikes, increases mouse lifespan, we studied ACA at three doses: 400, 1,000 (the original dose),
and 2,500 ppm, using genetically heterogeneous mice at three sites. Each dose led to a significant
change (by log-rank test) in both sexes, with larger effects in males, consistent with the original
report. There were no significant differences among the three doses. The two higher doses produced
16% or 17% increases in median longevity of males, but only 4% or 5% increases in females. Age at
the 90th percentile was increased significantly (8%-11%) in males at each dose, but was
significantly increased (3%) in females only at 1,000 ppm. The sex effect on longevity is not
explained simply by weight or fat mass, which were reduced by ACA more in females than in males.
ACA at 1,000 ppm reduced lung tumors in males, diminished liver degeneration in both sexes and
glomerulosclerosis in females, reduced blood glucose responses to refeeding in males, and improved
rotarod performance in aging females, but not males. Three other interventions were also tested:
ursolic acid, 2-(2-hydroxyphenyl) benzothiazole (HBX), and INT-767; none of these affected lifespan
at the doses tested. The acarbose results confirm and extend our original report, prompt further
attention to the effects of transient periods of high blood glucose on aging and the diseases of
aging, including cancer, and should motivate studies of acarbose and other glucose-control drugs
in humans."
- Note: acarbose started at 16 months (C2012 cohort) also extended male lifespan (MPD: p=0.0001).

### COMBINATIONS

**Strong R et al. Aging Cell 2022;21(12):e13724. doi:10.1111/acel.13724. PMC9741502**
"Lifespan benefits for the combination of rapamycin plus acarbose and for captopril in genetically
heterogeneous mice."
- Rapa 14.7 ppm + ACA 1000 ppm from 9 months: male median +34%, female median +28%, both p<0.0001.
  This is the largest ITP lifespan effect published.
- Same combination from 16 months: +13% both sexes, p<0.0001.
- Captopril 180 ppm from 5 months: male +14% (p=0.001), female +6% (p=0.002).
- Null in the same cohort: L-leucine 40,000 ppm, PB125, sulindac 5 ppm, syringaresinol 300 ppm.
  (R/S)-1,3-butanediol 100,000 ppm: female-only marginal (MPD p=0.0395).
- Authors' historical-comparison caveat, verbatim: "Each of these comparisons leads to the
  conclusion that the combination of Rapa and Aca leads to a greater extension of male lifespan
  than the same dose of Rapa by itself, although caution is always warranted when comparing among
  data sets produced in different years."

### 17-ALPHA-ESTRADIOL

- 4.8 ppm from 10 mo: male +12% (p=0.002) (Harrison 2014).
- 14.4 ppm from 10 mo: male median +19% (P<0.001); "17aE2 treatment had no significant effect on
  female survival" (Strong 2016).
- Late start (Harrison DE et al. Aging Cell 2021;20(5):e13328. PMC8135004): 14.4 ppm from
  16 months → "median male lifespans increased 19% (p < 0.0001, log-rank test)"; from 20 months
  → "11% (p = 0.007)". "17aE2 did not alter female lifespan."
- Corrigendum to the 2021 paper (Aging Cell 2022, PMC9649595) is an AUTHORSHIP correction only
  (adding Adrian Markewych as 11th author). No numbers or conclusions changed. Recording it so
  nobody mistakes it for a data correction.

### GLYCINE — one of the few both-sex hits, and it is small
**Miller RA et al. Aging Cell 2019;18(3):e12953. doi:10.1111/acel.12953. PMC6516426**
Abstract verbatim (key sentence): "Elevated glycine led to a small (4%-6%) but statistically
significant lifespan increase, as well as an increase in maximum lifespan, in both males
(p = 0.002) and females (p < 0.001). Pooling across sex, glycine increased lifespan at each of the
three independent sites, with significance at p = 0.01, 0.053, and 0.03, respectively."
- 8% glycine diet from 9 months. Male median +6.2%, female +3.7% (per results text).
- "End-of-life necropsies suggested that glycine-treated mice were less likely than controls to
  die of pulmonary adenocarcinoma (p = 0.03)."
- Supplement marketers quote glycine as an ITP hit without the 4-6% caliber.

### CANAGLIFLOZIN
**Miller RA et al. JCI Insight 2020;5(21):e140019. PMC7710304**
- 180 ppm from 7 months. Male median +14% (p<0.001); 90th percentile males +9% (p<0.001).
  Female median +1%, NS; no female maximum-lifespan benefit.
- ~50 M and ~50 F per site treated, ~100 controls per sex per site.
- Mechanism claim: benefit "is likely to reflect blunting of peak glucose levels" rather than
  average glucose; no effect on HbA1c.
- "The authors have declared that no conflict of interest exists." All-NIH/ADA funding.

### 2024 COHORT REPORT — first ITP result showing FEMALE HARM
**Miller RA, Harrison DE, et al. "Lifespan effects in male UM-HET3 mice treated with sodium
thiosulfate, 16-hydroxyestriol, and late-start canagliflozin." GeroScience 2024;46(5):4657-70.
doi:10.1007/s11357-024-01176-2. PMID 38753230**
- Cohort born 2020. Tested: alpha-ketoglutarate (AKG), 2,4-dinitrophenol (DNP), hydralazine,
  nebivolol, 16alpha-hydroxyestriol, sodium thiosulfate; plus canagliflozin started at 16 months.
- 16-OH-estriol: male median +15% (p=0.0001); FEMALE median **-7%**, significant decline.
- Late-start canagliflozin (16 mo): male +14% (p=0.004); FEMALE **-6%** (p=0.03).
- Canagliflozin from 6 months reproduced the male-only benefit with no female change,
  "suggesting that this agent may lead to female-specific late-life harm."
- Blood canagliflozin ~20-fold higher in aged females than young males.
- AKG (the compound sold as Rejuvant) 20,000 ppm from 18 months: NULL.
- TWO CORRECTIONS to this paper:
  * Correction 1: GeroScience 2025;47(4):6093-4. doi:10.1007/s11357-024-01283-0. PMID 39004654.
  * Correction 2: GeroScience 2025;47:6663-4. doi:10.1007/s11357-025-01752-0 — the compound was
    misnamed: five places in the paper called it "16-hydroxyestradiol" when it should have read
    "16-hydroxyestriol" (the two corrections run in opposite directions in their titles; the
    later 2025 correction's title reads "...16-hydroxyestradiol..."). Net effect: the ITP had to
    publish two notices to settle WHICH MOLECULE it tested. Worth flagging as a caution about
    how nailed-down even gold-standard preclinical reporting is.

**Follow-up pathology: Snyder JM et al. "End-of-life pathology in UM-HET3 mice treated with
16alpha-hydroxyestradiol or late-start canagliflozin." GeroScience 2025;48(2):1787-97.
doi:10.1007/s11357-025-01741-3. PMC12972294**
- Verbatim: "The analysis did not, however, reveal a clear pattern that would explain the
  selective sex-specific effects of either agent on lifespan."
- "It is plausible that the female-specific harm induced by each of these agents could reflect
  harmful or toxic effects that are not easily detectable by histopathological examination."

### 2024 REPORT — astaxanthin / meclizine hits + five nulls
**Harrison DE et al. GeroScience 2024;46(1):795-816. doi:10.1007/s11357-023-01011-0. PMID 38041783.
PMC10828146**
Abstract verbatim: "In genetically heterogeneous (UM-HET3) mice produced by the CByB6F1 × C3D2F1
cross, the Nrf2 activator astaxanthin (Asta) extended the median male lifespan by 12% (p = 0.003,
log-rank test), while meclizine (Mec), an mTORC1 inhibitor, extended the male lifespan by 8%
(p = 0.03). Asta was fed at 1840 ± 520 (9) ppm and Mec at 544 ± 48 (9) ppm, stated as mean ± SE (n)
of independent diet preparations. Both were started at 12 months of age. The 90th percentile
lifespan for both treatments was extended in absolute value by 6% in males, but neither was
significant by the Wang-Allison test. Five other new agents were also tested as follows: fisetin,
SG1002 (hydrogen sulfide donor), dimethyl fumarate, mycophenolic acid, and 4-phenylbutyrate.
None of these increased lifespan significantly at the dose and method of administration tested in
either sex."
- FISETIN, 600 ppm from 20 months: NULL both sexes. This is the flagship "senolytic" supplement.

### 2025 REPORT — epicatechin / halofuginone / mitoglitazone (male-only)
**GeroScience 2025, doi:10.1007/s11357-025-01881-6, online 19 Sep 2025.**
- Cohort bred 2021; all agents in diet ad libitum from 7 months.
  Tested: 2BAct, dichloroacetate, epicatechin, forskolin, halofuginone, mitoglitazone.
- Male median: epicatechin ~+5%; halofuginone ~+9%; mitoglitazone ~+9%.
  Epicatechin and halofuginone (not mitoglitazone) increased 90% survival. No female benefit.
- Verbatim: "This report continues the strong male bias in the efficacy of life-extending drugs
  identified so far."
- MPD p-values for this cohort: EPI m p=0.0370; HAL m p=0.0020; MIT m p=0.0150.
  2BAct, DCA, forskolin all null.

## 3. What the supplement industry sells that the ITP tested and that FAILED

All ITP-tested, all published null on lifespan in both sexes at the doses/schedules used:
- **Nicotinamide riboside (NR)** — 1000 ppm from 8 mo. "Lifespans for pooled data (across the
  three sites) were not significantly affected in UM-HET3 males or females fed diets with
  1,000 ppm nicotinamide riboside." Males p=0.252 (median -3%); females p=0.612 (median 0%).
  (Harrison 2021 Aging Cell, PMC8135004). This is the entire NAD+-precursor supplement category's
  only gold-standard mouse lifespan test, and it is a null.
- **Fisetin** — 600 ppm from 20 mo, null (GeroScience 2024). The senolytic supplement category.
- **Alpha-ketoglutarate (AKG / "Rejuvant")** — 20,000 ppm from 18 mo, null (2024); null again
  (2026).
- **Resveratrol** — 300 and 1200 ppm from 12 mo, null; 300 ppm from 4 mo, null (Miller 2011).
- **Curcumin** — 2000 ppm from 4 mo, null.
- **Green tea extract** — 2000 ppm from 4 mo, null by log-rank (female Gehan p marginal).
- **Fish oil** — 15,000 and 50,000 ppm from 9 mo, null.
- **MitoQ** — 100 ppm from 7 mo, null.
- **Oxaloacetate** — 2200 ppm from 4 mo, null.
- **Methylene blue** — 28 ppm from 4 mo, null.
- **Ursolic acid** — 2000 ppm from 10 mo, null (Harrison 2019, verbatim above).
- **Inulin** — 600 ppm from 11 mo, null.
- **L-leucine** — 40,000 ppm from 5 mo, null.
- **Astaxanthin** — HIT at 1840 ppm from 12 mo in males (+12%), then FAILED at other
  doses/start ages, and associated with significantly REDUCED female lifespan in the 2026 report.
- **Protandim** (a marketed Nrf2 supplement) — male-only +7% median (P<0.012), no female effect.
  The only marketed supplement blend with an ITP male hit, and it is 7%.
- **Glycine** — real but 4-6%.

## 4. Sex asymmetry — the pattern and the ITP's own explanation

Pattern: of the compounds that pass log-rank, the large majority are male-only
(acarbose, 17aE2, NDGA, canagliflozin, captopril[both but M>>F], astaxanthin, meclizine,
epicatechin, halofuginone, mitoglitazone, 16-OH-estriol, aspirin, Protandim, and metformin's
Gehan-only signal). Rapamycin is the conspicuous exception and favours FEMALES.
Glycine and Rapa+ACA benefit both.

ITP investigators' own stated explanations:
1. **Pharmacokinetics.** Rapamycin: "It seems likely that this differential effect, seen now in
   three consecutive independent cohorts, reflects higher levels of rapamycin in blood of female
   UM-HET3 mice compared with males." (Miller 2014). Canagliflozin: blood levels ~20-fold higher
   in aged females than young males — and canagliflozin HARMS old females. So PK asymmetry cuts
   both ways.
2. **Gonadal hormones.** Garratt M, Bower B, Garcia GG, Miller RA. "Sex differences in lifespan
   extension with acarbose and 17-alpha estradiol: gonadal hormones underlie male-specific
   improvements in glucose tolerance and mTORC2 signaling." Aging Cell 2017;16(6):1256-66.
   PMC5676051. Castration/ovariectomy at 3 months, drug from 4 months.
   Verbatim: "In contrast to intact males, castrated males showed no significant improvement in
   their ability to clear glucose after ACA or 17aE2 treatment when tested at 22 months of age."
   Conclusion verbatim: "male gonads, probably via testosterone production, contribute to these
   sexually dimorphic metabolic responses, with castrated males showing the lack of drug response
   typical of intact females." Funding: Glenn Foundation for Medical Research, NIH AG024824,
   AG022303.
3. **Countervailing harm in females**, not absence of benefit. NDGA: authors hypothesise it
   "produces beneficial effects in both sexes but produces harmful effects in females that
   prevent lifespan extension" (Harrison 2014). The 2024 and 2026 reports make this concrete —
   16-OH-estriol, late-start canagliflozin, astaxanthin, pioglitazone all SHORTENED female life.
4. **Statistical floor.** Control females live longer than control males (887 vs 803 days,
   Cheng 2019) at two of three sites, so there is less headroom in females — but the ITP itself
   notes rapamycin still helps females MORE, which argues against headroom being the whole story.
5. Bluntly, verbatim from the 2025 report: "This report continues the strong male bias in the
   efficacy of life-extending drugs identified so far." The ITP has not resolved the mechanism.

## 5. Critiques and limits

**External, on effect-size inflation:**
Pabis K, Barardo D, Gruber J, Sirbu O, Malavolta M, Selvarajoo K, Kaeberlein M, Kennedy BK.
"The impact of short-lived controls on the interpretation of lifespan experiments and progress in
geroscience - Through the lens of the '900-day rule'." Ageing Res Rev 2024;101:102512.
doi:10.1016/j.arr.2024.102512. No COI declared.
Abstract verbatim: "Our reanalysis of lifespan studies from multiple sources suggests that short
lifespans in the control group exaggerate the relative efficacy of putative longevity
interventions. Results may be exaggerated due to statistical effects (e.g. regression to the mean)
or other factors. Moreover, due to the high cost and long timeframes of mouse studies, it is rare
that a particular longevity intervention will be independently replicated by multiple groups...
In the absence of independent replication, a putative mouse longevity intervention should only be
considered with high confidence when control median lifespans are close to 900 days or if the
final lifespan of the treated group is considerably above 900 days."
- Applied to the ITP: control females at 887 days sit essentially AT the 900-day threshold, and
  control males at 803 days sit below it. So ITP female results are the ones that clear the bar
  most cleanly; male-only hits sit on a control baseline ~100 days below the strain ceiling.
  Note Kaeberlein and Kennedy are co-authors on the 2026 ITP paper — this is a friendly critique
  from inside the field, not an outside attack.

**Internal, from the ITP itself:**
- Site heterogeneity is real and unfixable: "the ITP has consistently found that control male mice
  at one site weigh less and live longer than the control males at the other two sites, even though
  each site uses the same food preparations and standardized husbandry."
- Cross-cohort comparison is explicitly hedged: "caution is always warranted when comparing among
  data sets produced in different years" (Strong 2022).
- The 2026 report's own conclusion is that "timing and dosage are critical variables" and that
  multi-site verification is required before believing a single cohort — i.e. even a positive ITP
  cohort is not final.
- Post-hoc test choice matters: Jiang 2024 shows five compounds flip from null to significant if
  you swap log-rank for Gehan, with the authors themselves flagging inflated type-1 error.

**Structural limits I could NOT find the ITP addressing:**
- All mice are the SAME cross (UM-HET3). "Genetically heterogeneous" removes strain idiosyncrasy
  within one 4-way cross; it does not sample mouse genetic diversity broadly.
- Barrier / clean-ish housing, sterilized NIH-31 chow, ad libitum feeding, no predation, no
  pathogen load, no cold, no exercise demand. Every hit is a hit *in that environment*.
- Ad libitum-fed controls are metabolically unlike a healthy human; several ITP hits
  (acarbose, canagliflozin, rapamycin, metformin) act on glucose/nutrient handling, so part of the
  benefit may be correcting ad-lib overfeeding rather than slowing aging.
- No ITP compound has ever been tested for lifespan in a second species by the ITP.
  (The NIA runs a separate Caenorhabditis Intervention Testing Program; not the same compounds.)
- The ITP measures LIFESPAN. Healthspan/function measures are secondary and inconsistently
  reported across cohorts.

## 6. Magnitude framing (for the article)

Reference frame: UM-HET3 control median = 803 d (M), 887 d (F) (Cheng 2019, n=3690 controls).
- Best single agent, male: acarbose +16-17% median (~+130 d on 803).
- Best single agent, female: rapamycin 42 ppm +26% median (~+230 d on 887).
- Best combination: rapamycin + acarbose from 9 mo, +34% M / +28% F.
- Best late-life start: rapamycin 42 ppm from 20 mo, +11% M / +15% F. 17aE2 from 20 mo, +11% M.
- Typical "hit": 5-14%. Marketed supplements that hit: Protandim +7% M; glycine +4-6% both.
- The famous Nature 2009 headline number (14% F / 9% M) is a 90th-percentile figure from a
  600-day start, i.e. one of the SMALLER rapamycin results in the ITP's own ledger, not the
  largest.
- Everything above is in mice, on lifespan, in a barrier facility. No ITP result is a human
  clinical outcome. Nothing in the ITP measures a biomarker clock.
