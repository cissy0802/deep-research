# L3 — Metformin: the epidemiology that launched it, the reversal, and TAME

Research agent working notes. Compiled 2026-07-28. All numbers traced to primary sources
(paper PDF / journal full text / ClinicalTrials.gov API) except where explicitly flagged.

---

## 0. The one-line shape of this line

A single 2014 observational study on a UK primary-care database — funded by
AstraZeneca/Bristol-Myers Squibb, with one author employed by BMS — reported that
diabetics on metformin outlived matched non-diabetics. It was never replicated. Two
independent attempts (Denmark 2022, Wales 2023) found the opposite. The mouse lifespan
trial (ITP) was null. The only long randomised human evidence in non-diabetics (DPPOS,
21 years) is flatly null on both mortality and cardiovascular events. The trial designed
to settle it (TAME) has never enrolled a patient, is not registered on ClinicalTrials.gov,
and its architect said on the record in 2022 that he had been predicting its imminent
start "for the last seven years."

Tier discipline for this line:
- Tier 1 (animal): ITP null; Cell 2024 monkey study n=6 treated, clock endpoints only.
- Tier 2 (human surrogate): MILES n=14 gene expression; METFORAGING n=40 epigenetic age, null.
- Tier 3 (human clinical outcome): DPP/DPPOS — null. VA-IMPACT — running, reads out 2029.
The hype consists almost entirely of promoting tier-1 and tier-2 results to tier-3 language.

## 1. Bannister et al. 2014 — the origin claim

**Citation.** Bannister CA, Holden SE, Jenkins-Jones S, Morgan CLl, Halcox JP, Schernthaner G,
Mukherjee J, Currie CJ. "Can people with type 2 diabetes live longer than those without? A
comparison of mortality in people initiated with metformin or sulphonylurea monotherapy and
matched, non-diabetic controls." Diabetes Obes Metab 2014. doi:10.1111/dom.12354.
Full text read from PDF: https://gwern.net/doc/longevity/metformin/2014-bannister.pdf

**Design.**
- Data source: UK Clinical Practice Research Datalink (CPRD), primary care. Data to July 2013.
  Incident T2D from 2000. CPRD ISAC approval ref 12_151RAR.
- Groups: 78,241 metformin monotherapy; 12,222 sulphonylurea monotherapy; 78,241 + 12,222 =
  90,463 matched non-diabetic controls.
- Matching variables (verbatim): "age at baseline (±2 years), gender, same general practice,
  prior cancer status and smoking status."
- Total censored follow-up 503,384 person-years. Follow-up **mean 2.8 / median 2.4 years**.
- Survival analysis truncated at **5½ years**. Start of survival clock = index date + 180 days.
  Both arms required ≥180 days survival/treatment.
- Model: parametric accelerated failure time, log-logistic (Cox rejected — PH assumption
  violated). Output = survival time ratio (STR), a ratio of *median* survival times.

**Headline numbers (verbatim from abstract).**
- Unadjusted mortality rates per 1000 person-years: metformin **14.4**, its matched controls
  **15.2**; sulphonylurea **50.9**, its matched controls **28.7**.
- "adjusted median survival time was 15% lower (STR = 0.85, 95% CI 0.81–0.90) in matched
  individuals without diabetes and 38% lower (0.62, 0.58–0.66) in diabetic patients treated
  with sulphonylurea monotherapy."
- Conclusion verbatim: "Patients with type 2 diabetes initiated with metformin monotherapy had
  longer survival than did matched, non-diabetic controls… This supports the position of
  metformin as first-line therapy and implies that metformin may confer benefit in non-diabetes."
- Final sentence of the paper: "Intriguingly, these findings suggest that there may be a
  prognostic benefit of metformin prophylaxis in people without diabetes."

**Caliber problems visible in the paper itself.**
- The metformin arm was *more* comorbid at baseline: BMI 32.4 vs 27.4 kg/m²; Charlson 1.9 vs
  0.7; prior MACE 10% vs 6%; GP contacts in prior year 11.3 vs 6.0; prior lipid-lowering 50%
  vs 20%. The paper calls the result "Surprisingly… despite greater morbidity."
- **BMI, HbA1c, systolic BP, cholesterol and creatinine were NOT adjusted for.** Verbatim:
  "Glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c), systolic blood pressure, total cholesterol, creatinine and
  BMI were not considered because of large proportions of missing data in controls."
  So the single largest confounder distinguishing the arms (BMI, 5 kg/m² apart) is in the
  model as nothing at all.
- **Informative censoring.** Censor date defined as earliest of end of data, **"90 days from
  regimen change,"** or 5 years + 180 days. Metformin patients who progressed to add-on
  therapy — i.e. those whose diabetes was worsening — were censored out. Controls were not
  censored for any analogous reason. This systematically removes deteriorating patients from
  the metformin arm.
- **Truncation at 5.5 years.** Rationale given: "the average duration of first-line
  monotherapy was 3 years." Consequence: the study cannot see anything past 5.5 years — which
  is exactly where the Welsh 20-year replication finds the effect reverses (§4).

**Authors' own stated limitations (verbatim).**
- "As this was an observational study, patients were not randomized to treatment, and
  uncharacterized confounders may account for some of the differences between groups."
- "However, we could not adjust for some parameters due to the understandably high percentage
  of missing data in controls."
- "Therefore, it is probable that some controls had undiagnosed type 2 diabetes."
- "people with type 2 diabetes are more likely to be receiving exercise and lifestyle
  interventions and close monitoring and control of blood pressure and cholesterol levels…
  Therefore, these conditions may be less well diagnosed in the control group."
  (i.e. the authors themselves name surveillance/treatment asymmetry as an explanation.)
- Also verbatim, on the metformin-vs-sulphonylurea contrast: "this needs to be interpreted
  with caution because we did not adjust for important covariates because they were
  unavailable in the non-diabetic subjects."

**Funding / COI (verbatim).**
- Acknowledgements: "This article was reviewed for scientific content and supported by
  AstraZeneca/Bristol-Myers Squibb."
- COI: "C. A. B. and C. Ll. M. are contractors of, S. E. H. and S. J.-J. are employed by and
  C. J. C. is a director of Pharmatelligence, a research consultancy receiving funding from
  pharmaceutical companies. J. M. is an employee of Bristol-Myers Squibb." Currie's
  consultancy list includes AstraZeneca, BMS, Lilly, MSD, Sanofi-Aventis, Takeda etc.
- Context worth noting: AZ/BMS were the marketers of the metformin-containing combination
  products (Kombiglyze/Komboglyze) and of saxagliptin at the time; the paper's other
  conclusion — that sulphonylureas are harmful — is commercially convenient for a company
  selling non-sulphonylurea second-line agents.

## 2. The methodological critiques

### 2a. Immortal time bias / time-window bias / time-lag bias — Suissa & Azoulay
Suissa S, Azoulay L. "Metformin and the risk of cancer: time-related biases in observational
studies." Diabetes Care 2012;35:2665–2673. PMID 23173135.
- Reviewed 24 influential metformin-cancer studies: **13 had immortal time bias, 9 had
  time-window bias, 3 had time-lag bias.**
- Mechanism, verbatim gist: the interval between cohort entry and first metformin prescription
  is "immortal" — the patient must survive to receive it — and is misclassified as exposed time.
- Follow-up: Suissa S. "Metformin and Cancer: Solutions to a Real-World Evidence Failure."
  Diabetes Care 2023;46(5):904.

### 2b. Direct demonstration of how much the bias is worth (2025)
Khouri C, Dell'Aniello S, Yin H, Azoulay L, Suissa S. "Metformin to treat breast cancer: an
application of the prevalent new-user design for observational studies with no active
comparator." J Clin Epidemiol 2025. PMID 40578405.
- CPRD, 13,314 women with incident breast cancer + T2D, 1998–2020. 4,923 metformin initiators
  matched 1:1 to 4,923 nonusers by time-conditional propensity score.
- Properly designed: **all-cause mortality HR 0.96 (95% CI 0.89–1.05)**; breast-cancer
  mortality HR 1.12 (0.98–1.28). Null.
- Same data, flawed design ignoring immortal time: reproduces the "implausible beneficial
  effects" reported in the older literature.
- This is the cleanest available demonstration that the metformin survival signal is an
  artefact of design, in the same database Bannister used.

### 2c. Healthy-adherer effect
Simpson SH, Eurich DT, Majumdar SR, et al. "A meta-analysis of the association between
adherence to drug therapy and mortality." BMJ 2006;333:15. PMID 16790458.
- 21 studies, 46,847 participants; 8 placebo arms, 19,633 participants.
- Good vs poor adherence: mortality OR **0.56 (95% CI 0.50–0.63)**.
- **Good adherence to PLACEBO: OR 0.56 (0.43–0.74)** — statistically indistinguishable from
  good adherence to active drug (0.55, 0.49–0.62).
- Implication for metformin: in a cohort defined by "received treatment for a minimum of 180
  days" (Bannister's inclusion rule), roughly the entire observed survival advantage is
  within the size of the adherence artefact. The non-diabetic controls were not selected for
  adherence to anything.
- Original observation: Coronary Drug Project, NEJM 1980 — placebo adherers had lower mortality.

### 2d. Never-diabetic control selection (named by Keys 2022/2025)
Verbatim from Keys et al. 2025: matched control groups in Bannister "were selected as being
'never-diabetic' by conditioning across the full range of future follow-up. This can
potentially artificially increase mortality in the control group." Because people who are on
a trajectory to diabetes (obese, metabolic syndrome, high CV risk) are excluded from the
control pool by a *future* criterion, the control group is depleted of the healthy-but-
at-risk and enriched for people who die of non-diabetes causes early.

### 2e. Comparator (sulphonylurea) confounding
Bannister's sulphonylurea arm was 6.6 years older (67.8 vs 61.2), had higher HbA1c (9.2 vs
8.6%), higher creatinine (97.9 vs 84.2 µmol/l), higher Charlson (2.3 vs 1.9), more prior
cancer (14 vs 10%) and more prior MACE (16 vs 10%). Sulphonylureas are prescribed when
metformin is contraindicated — i.e. in renal impairment, which is itself a mortality
predictor. The metformin-vs-sulphonylurea contrast is confounding by indication almost by
construction. Bannister's own text concedes the covariate adjustment could not be done.

## 3. The reversal — Denmark

**Citation.** Keys MT, Thinggaard M, Larsen LA, Pedersen DA, Hallas J, Christensen K.
"Reassessing the evidence of a survival advantage in Type 2 diabetes treated with metformin
compared with controls without diabetes: a retrospective cohort study." Int J Epidemiol
2022;51(6):1886–1898. doi:10.1093/ije/dyac200. PMID 36287641.

**Design.** Danish National Health Registers 1996–2012. Source populations: 445,662 singletons
(5% random national sample) + 151,091 individual twins from the Danish Twin Registry.
Three designs run in parallel: matched cohort, nested case–control (44,629 singleton deaths
matched 1:1), and **discordant same-sex twin pairs** (976 pairs) — the twin design controls
for genetics, birth year, sex and shared family environment, which no other study in this
literature does.

**Matched cohort:** 7,842 metformin initiators vs 7,842 matched without diabetes.
Median follow-up 3.27 years (mean 4.20).

**Results — every direction is opposite to Bannister.**
| Comparison | Unadjusted | Fully adjusted |
|---|---|---|
| Singleton matched cohort, HR | 1.48 (1.32–1.64) | 1.33 (1.16–1.54) |
| Singleton nested case–control, IRR | 1.52 (1.37–1.68) | 1.32 (1.16–1.50) |
| Discordant twin pairs, HR | 2.15 (1.48–3.13) | 1.80 (1.11–2.91) |
| Discordant twin pairs, IRR | 1.90 (1.35–2.67) | 1.64 (1.10–2.46) |

Crude mortality/1000 PY: metformin 24.93 (23.23–26.64) vs matched non-diabetic 16.86
(15.46–18.27). Twins: 21.68 vs 10.08.
8-year survival: 0.83 metformin vs 0.88 controls (singletons); 0.83 vs 0.93 (twins).
Increased mortality held **across all levels of cumulative use** (1–200, 201–500, 501–1000,
1000+ DDD) — i.e. no dose–response protection.

**The killer forensic comparison (verbatim from the discussion).** Keys et al. put their own
crude rates side by side with Bannister's:
- non-diabetic controls matched to metformin: **16.9 (Denmark) vs 15.2 (Bannister)** — nearly identical
- non-diabetic controls matched to sulfonylurea: **28.4 vs 28.7** — nearly identical
- sulfonylurea initiators: **49.0 vs 50.9** — nearly identical
- **metformin initiators: 24.9 (Denmark) vs 14.4 (Bannister)** — the ONLY discrepant cell.
Three of four cells replicate to within a few percent. The anomaly is entirely inside
Bannister's metformin arm. That is the signature of a selection/censoring artefact in that
arm specifically, not of a population difference.

Verbatim conclusion: "Treatment initiation by metformin monotherapy in Type 2 diabetes was not
associated with survival equal or superior to that of the general population without diabetes.
Our contrasting findings compared with previous research are unlikely to be the result of
differences in epidemiological or methodological parameters. Further research is necessary
before interpreting this line of research as supporting evidence for the anti-ageing or
geroprotective potential of metformin."

They also state that they ran sensitivity analyses reproducing Bannister's potential biases
(informative censoring of metformin initiators, disadvantageous selection into the control
group) and these "likely had only marginal impact… these differences did not explain the
extent of the contrast observed in our study."

**Funding/COI.** Velux Foundation grant 31205 to Kaare Christensen, Danish Ageing Research
Center. "Conflict of interest: None declared." Note the asymmetry with Bannister: the
positive study was industry-funded, the null replication was foundation-funded with no COI.

## 4. The reversal — Wales (why the truncation mattered)

**Citation.** Stevenson-Hoare J, Leonenko G, Escott-Price V. "Comparison of long-term effects
of metformin on longevity between people with type 2 diabetes and matched non-diabetic
controls." BMC Public Health 2023 May 2;23(1):804. doi:10.1186/s12889-023-15764-y. PMID 37131166.

- Secure Anonymised Information Linkage (SAIL) dataset, Wales UK — the *same country and the
  same kind of primary-care record system* as Bannister.
- N = **129,140 metformin**, 68,563 sulphonylurea, matched non-diabetic controls on sex, age,
  smoking, history of cancer and cardiovascular disease.
- Analysed over "a range of simulated study periods" up to 20 years.

**Verbatim findings:** "Using the full twenty-year period, we found that type 2 diabetes
patients treated with metformin had shorter survival time than matched controls, as did
sulphonylurea patients. Metformin patients had better survival than sulphonylurea patients,
controlling for age. **Within the first three years, metformin therapy showed a benefit over
matched controls, but this reversed after five years of treatment.**"

Interpretation verbatim: "While metformin does appear to confer benefits to longevity in the
short term, these initial benefits are outweighed by the effects of type 2 diabetes when
patients are observed over a period of up to twenty years. Longer study periods are therefore
recommended for studying longevity and healthy lifespan."

**This is the decisive structural point of the whole line.** Bannister truncated at 5.5 years
and reported a benefit. A study in the same country with a larger cohort finds a benefit
inside 3 years that *reverses* after 5. Bannister's analytic window sat exactly on the segment
of the curve where the artefact lives. A claim about *longevity* was built on a 2.4-year
median follow-up.

Note: PubMed abstract does not report HRs/CIs at the 3/5/20-year timepoints — I could not
reach the paywalled full text, so those point estimates are a gap.

## 5. 2024–2026 state of play: target trial emulation

### The one positive TTE — and what it actually compares
**Citation.** Shadyab AH, Espeland MA, Odegaard AO, Manson JE, Johnson KC, Chen Z, Zhu B,
LaCroix AZ, Howard B, et al. "Comparative Effectiveness of Metformin Versus Sulfonylureas on
Exceptional Longevity in Women With Type 2 Diabetes: Target Trial Emulation."
J Gerontol A Biol Sci Med Sci 2025;80(7):glaf095.

- Women's Health Initiative. New-user, active-comparator, 1:1 propensity-score matched.
- **N = 438 total (219 metformin, 219 sulfonylurea).** Postmenopausal women, ≥60 years,
  mean age 70.0 at initiation, 84.9% White.
- Outcome: death before age 90. Incidence 3.7 vs 5.0 per 100 person-years.
- **Adjusted HR 0.70 (95% CI 0.56–0.88).** E-value 1.88.
- Verbatim conclusion: "In this first target trial emulation of metformin and exceptional
  longevity, we found that metformin initiation increased exceptional longevity compared with
  sulfonylurea initiation among women with type 2 diabetes."
- Verbatim caveat: "Because this comparison was not made to placebo in a randomized controlled
  trial and given the observational design with potential for residual confounding, causality
  cannot be inferred."
- Funding: NHLBI/NIH (75N92021D00001–75N92021D00005). Most authors no COI; B.H. lecture fees.

**Caliber.** The comparator is **sulfonylurea, not non-diabetics and not placebo.** N=438,
one sex, one age band. An E-value of 1.88 means an unmeasured confounder of modest strength
(RR ~1.9 on both exposure and outcome) would null it — and BMI/renal function differ between
metformin and sulfonylurea initiators by roughly that much. This study is fully compatible
with "sulfonylureas are harmful" and says nothing about metformin vs no drug. It cannot
support the Bannister claim; it does not even test it.

### Other TTE work on my line
- Scottish nationwide TTE of stopping vs continuing metformin in advanced CKD (AJKD 2024,
  Am J Kidney Dis S0272-6386(24)01041-2 / eprints.gla.ac.uk/341933) — landmark design at
  6 months post CKD G4/G5; a different question (safety of continuation), not longevity.
- I searched specifically for a 2024–2026 target trial emulation that re-runs the
  Bannister comparison (metformin initiators vs matched non-diabetics, mortality) and
  **found none.** See gaps.

## 6. TAME — design, endpoint, budget, and actual status

### Design (as published/described by AFAR and Barzilai)
- ~3,000 adults aged **65–79**, **14 sites**, coordinating centre Wake Forest University
  School of Medicine, ~**6 years**. Designed by Nir Barzilai and colleagues at Albert
  Einstein College of Medicine; managed/fundraised by the American Federation for Aging
  Research (AFAR).
- Origin paper: Barzilai N, Crandall JP, Kritchevsky SB, Espeland MA. "Metformin as a Tool to
  Target Aging." Cell Metab 2016;23(6):1060–1065. PMID 27304507. (Cell.com and ScienceDirect
  both 403'd for me — full text not reached; design details below are from AFAR + Barzilai's
  own 2025 interview.)
- **Composite primary endpoint**, Barzilai verbatim (Lifespan.io, 2025-08-12): "TAME is
  designed to measure a cluster of outcomes; it doesn't look at mortality independently. The
  primary endpoint is a cluster of cardiovascular disease, cognitive decline, cancer, and then
  mortality. So, you're not going to get a single mortality number out of it."
- Why the composite mattered regulatorily: ageing is not an FDA indication, so no trial can
  have "ageing" as an endpoint. The composite (time to first of MI/stroke/heart failure/
  cancer/dementia/death) operationalises ageing as a measurable morbidity substrate without
  requiring the FDA to recognise ageing as a disease. TAME investigators worked with FDA
  around 2015 on this. Barzilai verbatim on the point of the trial (STAT 2022): "There's
  nothing in that clinical trial that hasn't already been shown before with metformin. We are
  just trying to package it all up and call it aging so the FDA will approve this for aging."
  — i.e. the architect describes TAME as a *regulatory* exercise, not a discovery exercise.

### Budget
- STAT (2022-08-09): trial requires **$30–50 million**; NIH provided ~$9 million for
  biomarker work only. Other secondary sources give $50–75M. A private foundation reportedly
  offered to fund about a third. Barzilai has cited a burn of roughly $8M/year for 4–5 years.
  **I could not reach a primary AFAR budget document; treat the exact figure as uncertain.**

### ACTUAL STATUS as of 2026-07-28 — the load-bearing finding
1. **TAME is not registered on ClinicalTrials.gov.** I queried the ClinicalTrials.gov API v2
   three ways — `query.titles=Targeting Aging with Metformin`, `query.term=metformin aging TAME`,
   and an `AREA[OfficialTitle]/AREA[BriefTitle]` TAME query restricted to intervention=metformin.
   None returned any 3,000-participant Wake Forest/AFAR trial. Registration is a prerequisite
   for enrolling a US participant. **Therefore TAME has enrolled zero participants.**
2. **AFAR's own TAME page (fetched 2026-07-28) is still written entirely in the future tense
   and in fundraising language.** It asks for "Visionary donors to help launch the multi-year,
   multi-site TAME Trial" and says "AFAR is leading fundraising efforts to support and launch
   the TAME Trial." It states the trial "will engage over 3,000 individuals." There is no
   launch date, no enrolment figure, no NCT number.
3. **Barzilai's own most recent public statement (Lifespan.io interview, published
   2025-08-12), verbatim:** "I cannot give you a perfect update because it's now being handled
   within ARPA-H. I think there will be two major trials that come out of this. One is going
   to be from Eli Lilly; they're going to do a TAME-like study but with their GLP-1 agonist…
   This conversation is ongoing, and we're holding everything because I would love for all
   four major drug classes to be tested so we can get comparisons." He calls TAME
   "long-awaited" and does not claim it has started.
4. **How long it has been "about to start" — Barzilai on the record, STAT 2022-08-09:**
   "I'm on the record saying I'm pretty sure it will start this year, for the last seven
   years. I have no credibility anymore. It's just incredibly frustrating. But it's going to
   happen, because it has to happen."
   Counting from the 2015 FDA meeting: **TAME has been imminent for ~11 years as of 2026.**
5. Also from STAT: "Those big billionaires, they want moonshots, they want a scientific
   achievement that will make people say 'wow.' TAME is not a moonshot."

### The structural reason it never started
Metformin is generic (US FDA approval of Glucophage 1995; compound dates to 1950s). No sponsor
can recoup a nine-figure outcome trial on an unpatentable molecule; public funders have been
reluctant to fund a trial whose endpoint is an indication that does not exist. This
circularity is precisely what TAME was designed to break, and it is what has prevented TAME
from being run. Barzilai's 2025 framing is that TAME's value is now as a *template* for
patented drugs: "the most important thing about TAME is that it's a template for the
pharmaceutical industry, and that's probably why Lilly is interested."

**NOT VERIFIED — do not use without further checking:** claims circulating on
AI-generated content farms that ARPA-H made a large longevity investment in Feb 2026, that a
"$38 million VITAL-H trial" of rapamycin/dapagliflozin/semaglutide is US-government funded,
and that TAME enrolment is "ongoing" as of early 2026. Barzilai's ARPA-H remark (Aug 2025) is
the only sourced part. My web-search budget ran out before I could verify VITAL-H on
ClinicalTrials.gov. Flagging for the fact-check pass.

### Barzilai COI
Founder/co-founder of CohBar Inc.; medical advisor to Life Biosciences; president of the
Academy for Health & Lifespan Research; AFAR board member (AFAR is the body fundraising for
TAME). I did not find a formal machine-readable disclosure statement to quote verbatim —
recorded here as an unquoted COI note, not as a claim.

## 7. The counter-evidence: metformin blunts exercise adaptation

### Konopka et al. 2019 (aerobic)
Konopka AR, Laurin JL, Schoenberg HM, Reid JJ, Castor WM, Wolff CA, Musci RV, Safairad OD,
Linden MA, Biela LM, Bailey SM, Hamilton KL, Miller BF. "Metformin inhibits mitochondrial
adaptations to aerobic exercise training in older adults." Aging Cell 2019;18(1):e12880.
PMC6351883.
- **N = 53 randomised (26 placebo, 27 metformin), 53 completed.** Age 62 ± 1 years.
- Metformin titrated 500 mg/wk to **2,000 mg/day** by week 4; 11 participants reduced to
  1,500 mg/day for GI intolerance. **12 weeks** of aerobic exercise training.
- VO2max: metformin "attenuated the increase in VO2max following 12 weeks of AET by ~50%,
  although this did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.08)."
- Mitochondrial respiration: metformin "abolished the ~25% increase in maximal CI-linked
  respiration after AET during the ADP titration."
- Insulin sensitivity: metformin attenuated the exercise-induced increase in peripheral
  insulin sensitivity by 30–50%.
- Conclusion verbatim: "Our findings suggest that combining two healthspan extending
  treatments, metformin and exercise, may interfere with the improvement in some parameters of
  physiological function and do not interact synergistically."
- Funding: **Dairy Management, Inc.** (to BFM & KLH) and **Dexcom, Inc.** (to ARK). No COI
  declared. (Odd funders for an exercise-physiology trial; worth a footnote.)
- Caliber note: the headline VO2max effect was **not statistically significant (p=0.08)**.
  Do not report it as a significant finding.

### Walton et al. 2019 — the MASTERS trial (resistance)
Walton RG, Dungan CM, Long DE, et al. "Metformin blunts muscle hypertrophy in response to
progressive resistance exercise training in older adults: A randomized, double-blind,
placebo-controlled, multicenter trial: The MASTERS trial." Aging Cell 2019;18:e13039.
PMC6826125.
- **Randomised 109 (55 placebo, 54 metformin); completed 94 (48 placebo, 46 metformin).**
  Age 64.4–91.2 years, median 69.3. Metformin **1,700 mg/day**. **14 weeks** supervised
  progressive resistance training, multicentre.
- Lean body mass: placebo **+1.95%** (SD 2.69, p<.001); metformin **+0.41%** (SD 2.25,
  p=.218); **between-group p = .003**.
- Thigh muscle mass: placebo **+3.90%** (SD 5.54, p<.001); metformin **+0.45%** (SD 3.95,
  p=.441); **between-group p < .001**.
- Type II fibre CSA: placebo +18.5% vs metformin +14.5%, **p = .610** (not different) — so the
  whole-muscle effect is not cleanly explained at the fibre level. Report both.
- Conclusion verbatim: "These results underscore the benefits of PRT in older adults, but
  metformin negatively impacts the hypertrophic response to resistance training in healthy
  older individuals."
- The authors' *prior hypothesis was the opposite* — they expected metformin to augment the
  response via reduced inflammation. Prespecified direction was wrong; this is a genuine
  surprise, not a fishing expedition.
- Funding: NIA R01AG046920; NCATS UL1TR001998, UL1TR003096. "The authors have no conflict of
  interests to disclose." Public money, no industry.
- **CORRECTION EXISTS.** Corrigendum, Aging Cell (PMC7059173): "In Table 2, data in row
  Relative strength (kg/kg)c mean (SD) were incorrect." Corrected relative strength: placebo
  baseline 3.89 (1.48) → 4.62 (1.74), +18.9% (19.1); metformin 4.60 (1.39) → 5.23 (1.58),
  +14.8% (18.3); p=0.30. Appendix S2 also updated. The primary mass outcomes were not
  affected. Cite the corrected strength numbers, not the original ones.
- Follow-up transcriptomics: "Metformin alters skeletal muscle transcriptome adaptations to
  resistance training in older adults," PMC7655218 / PMID 33071237.

### Newer mechanistic confirmation
"Metformin suppresses the mitochondrial and transcriptional response to exercise, revealing a
conserved BCL6B-associated angiogenic program." J Appl Physiol 2025, doi
10.1152/japplphysiol.00432.2025. (Found via search; full text not fetched. Direction is
consistent with Konopka.)

**Why this matters for the article.** Barzilai concedes the point directly (2025-08-12):
"Whoever takes metformin who doesn't have diabetes and is not at least 50 years old is making
a mistake, in particular if they're trying to build their muscle or increase their VO2max."
So the strongest advocate agrees metformin has a real cost against the two interventions with
the best-established human outcome evidence (aerobic fitness, strength). That is a rare
point of agreement between advocate and sceptic and should be stated as such.

## 8. RCTs of metformin in non-diabetics for aging-related outcomes

### Tier 3 — hard clinical outcomes. Both null. This is the decisive human evidence.

**DPP/DPPOS cardiovascular.** Goldberg RB, et al. "Effects of Long-term Metformin and Lifestyle
Interventions on Cardiovascular Events in the Diabetes Prevention Program and its Outcome
Study." Circulation 2022;145(22):1632–1641. PMC9179081.
- 3,234 adults with impaired glucose tolerance randomised 1996–99: placebo n=1,073, metformin
  n=1,082 (850 mg BID), intensive lifestyle n=1,079. **Median follow-up 21 years.**
- Primary outcome: "First occurrence of nonfatal myocardial infarction, stroke, or
  cardiovascular death adjudicated by standard criteria."
- **Metformin vs placebo: HR 1.03 (95% CI 0.78–1.37; p=0.81).** 101 events vs 98.
- Extended CVD outcome: **HR 1.00 (0.80–1.25; p=0.99).**
- Lifestyle vs placebo: HR 1.14 (0.87–1.50).
- Conclusion verbatim: "Neither metformin nor lifestyle reduced major cardiovascular events in
  DPPOS over 21 years despite long-term prevention of diabetes."
- Funding NIDDK/NIH + CDC; Merck KGaA provided medication. Minimal COI.

**DPP/DPPOS mortality.** "Effect of Metformin and Lifestyle Interventions on Mortality in the
Diabetes Prevention Program and Diabetes Prevention Program Outcomes Study." Diabetes Care
2021;44(12):2775. PMID 34697033.
- Median 21 years; **453 deaths** (cancer 170, CVD 131).
- Metformin vs placebo: **all-cause HR 0.99 (0.79–1.25); cancer HR 1.04 (0.72–1.52);
  CVD HR 1.08 (0.70–1.66).** All null.
- Lifestyle vs placebo: all-cause 1.02 (0.81–1.28); cancer 1.07 (0.74–1.55); CVD 1.18 (0.77–1.81).
- (Numbers via search result quoting the abstract; the ADA site 403'd. I did not reach the
  full text — flag for verification.)
- Diabetes incidence over 21 years WAS reduced: lifestyle −24% (15–32), metformin −17%. So the
  drug did what it was licensed to do and still moved no hard outcome. That dissociation is
  the single most important fact on this line.

**Barzilai's rebuttal to DPPOS, verbatim (2025-08-12), for fairness:** "the study found that
metformin was good for you, so some people in the control group started taking it… Moving
forward, it wasn't a clinical study anymore; it became an observational study, and they didn't
find much… To make such a big story about the DPP is ridiculous." This is a real
intention-to-treat-dilution argument and should be stated. Counter-argument to state
alongside: crossover dilutes toward the null but the point estimates are 0.99 / 1.03 — dead
centre, not attenuated-but-positive — and a 21-year ITT null after a 3-year randomised
exposure is exactly the evidentiary standard the field applies to everything else.

**VA-IMPACT (NCT02915198)** — the only ongoing hard-outcome RCT of metformin in non-diabetics.
Verified directly against ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 on 2026-07-28:
- Title: Investigation of Metformin in Pre-Diabetes on Atherosclerotic Cardiovascular Outcomes.
- Lead sponsor: **VA Office of Research and Development.**
- **Status: RECRUITING.** Start date **2023-04-03**. Primary completion and completion date
  both **2029-09-28**. Enrollment **7,410 (estimated)**. Last update posted 2025-12-17.
- Primary outcome verbatim: "Time in days to death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, stroke,
  hospitalization for unstable angina, or symptom-driven coronary revascularization."
- Population: prediabetes + established ASCVD; metformin ER titrated to 2,000 mg/day vs placebo.
- **No results before 2029.** Anyone asserting metformin's cardiovascular benefit in
  prediabetes today is asserting it ahead of the trial designed to test it.

### Tier 2 — human surrogate endpoints

**MILES.** Kulkarni AS, Brutsaert EF, Anghel V, et al. "Metformin regulates metabolic and
nonmetabolic pathways in skeletal muscle and subcutaneous adipose tissues of older adults."
Aging Cell 2018;17(2):e12723. PMID 29383869.
- **N = 14**, ~70 years old, randomised double-blind placebo-controlled **crossover**,
  **6 weeks** each of metformin and placebo.
- Endpoints: gene expression from muscle and subcutaneous adipose biopsies + mixed-meal
  challenge. **647 genes differentially expressed in muscle, 146 in adipose at FDR<0.05.**
- Abstract verbatim: "these pleiotropic effects of metformin on the biology of human aging have
  not been studied." Authors claim "first evidence that, in older adults, metformin has
  metabolic and nonmetabolic effects linked to aging."
- **No clinical outcome measured. No aging outcome measured.** 14 people, 6 weeks, mRNA.
  This trial is routinely cited in popular longevity media as evidence metformin "slows aging
  in humans." It is a transcriptomic pilot.

**METFORAGING — the newest RCT, 2026, and it is null.**
Marcelo-Calvo C, Esteban-Cantos A, Jurado F, et al. "Metformin and epigenetic age in
non-diabetic older people with HIV in Madrid (METFORAGING): a double-blind, randomised,
placebo-controlled, pilot trial." EClinicalMedicine 2026;95:103874. PMID 42023167.
- Single-centre, La Paz University Hospital, Madrid. Non-diabetic, virally suppressed people
  with HIV, aged ≥50, CD4 >500.
- **Randomised 1:1, n=19 metformin / n=21 placebo (40 total); per-protocol 17/18 (35).**
  **850 mg twice daily for 96 weeks** — a long exposure by geroscience-trial standards.
- Primary outcome verbatim: "the adjusted between-group difference in epigenetic age
  acceleration (EAA) change measured by the PhenoAge clock at week 96 in the per-protocol
  population."
- Result verbatim: "At week 96, the adjusted between-group difference (metformin vs. placebo)
  for PhenoAge EAA was **−1.02 years (95% confidence interval [CI] −5.30 to 3.26; p = 0.627)**."
- Interpretation verbatim: "Although no significant difference was noted in the primary outcome
  between groups, these preliminary findings support the feasibility of geroscience-trials in
  this population and support further investigation of metformin in larger, adequately powered
  studies."
- **Caliber:** underpowered pilot (n=40), so this is not proof of no effect — the CI spans
  −5.3 to +3.3 years and cannot exclude a meaningful benefit. But it is the first randomised
  test of metformin against a clock endpoint in non-diabetics at 96 weeks, and it is null.
  Contrast with the uncontrolled/observational clock studies that report −3.5 year effects.

### Other non-diabetic trials, via Keys et al. 2025 (secondary; verify before use)
- **MA.32** (breast cancer adjuvant metformin RCT): "was not associated with improvements in
  overall (HR = 1.10) or progression-free survival."
- **CAMERA, MetCAB, GIPS-III**: "no effect" on their primary cardiovascular outcomes.
- **COVID-OUT**: "was without effect on its primary outcome" (reduced a tertiary outcome —
  this is the study Barzilai leans on hardest; he says the primary endpoint, hypoxia, was
  "silly"). **TOGETHER**: "no effect of metformin was observed against any primary or
  secondary outcome."
- **MET-PREVENT** (frailty): metformin "was poorly tolerated by the study population," no
  improvement in physical outcomes.

## 9. Animal evidence

### Mouse — ITP bottom line (L1 owns this in depth; one claim only)
Strong R, Miller RA, Antebi A, et al., NIA Interventions Testing Program. Aging Cell 2016;
15(5):872–884. PMC5013015.
- Genetically heterogeneous UM-HET3 mice, 3 sites. Metformin **1000 ppm (0.1%) in diet from
  9 months of age.** Pooled: **148 male and 140 female metformin mice vs 294 male and 281
  female controls.**
- **Metformin alone: +7% median lifespan in males, NOT significant (P = 0.35). No effect in
  females. No maximum-lifespan effect in either sex.**
- Keys et al. 2025 restate it as: metformin "failed to produce significant lifespan benefits
  in either male (n = 442) or female (n = 421) mice… No benefit of metformin was observed in
  either sex or in any secondary analysis." (Their n's are treated + concurrent controls.)
- The contrast that structures the whole article: rapamycin, tested in the same programme with
  the same mice and the same statistics, extends lifespan robustly and repeatedly. Metformin
  does not. Whatever the ITP is systematically bad at detecting, it detected rapamycin.
- Related 2025 meta-analysis: Ivimey-Cook et al., "Rapamycin, Not Metformin, Mirrors Dietary
  Restriction-Driven Lifespan Extension in Vertebrates: A Meta-Analysis." Aging Cell 2025;
  doi 10.1111/acel.70131. (Wiley 402'd; title alone carries the bottom line, but the effect
  sizes are a gap — do not quote numbers.)
- Barzilai's response, verbatim (2025-08-12): "There is a lifespan effect in mice, just not as
  much as with rapamycin. It's been recorded by 20-something studies, but Rich ignored
  something from his own ITP data… metformin is not for young people; it's only for old
  people." He references an unpublished-at-that-date ITP re-analysis by second half of life.
  **I could not locate that re-analysis; see gaps.**

### Monkey — the 2024 Cell study that got the "6 years younger brain" headlines
Yang Y, Lu X, Liu N, Ma S, Zhang H, Zhang Z, et al.; Qu J, Zhang W, Liu G-H (corresponding).
"Metformin decelerates aging clock in male monkeys." Cell 2024;187:6358–6378.e29, 31 October
2024. doi:10.1016/j.cell.2024.08.021. Full PDF read: gwern.net/doc/longevity/metformin/2024-yang.pdf

**What was actually done.**
- Male cynomolgus monkeys (*Macaca fascicularis*), **aged 13–16 years at start** ("roughly
  equivalent to approximately 40–50 years in humans"), **16–19 years at endpoint.**
- **Four groups. Sample sizes as reported in the figure legends: Y-Ctrl n = 6 (aged 3–5),
  M-Ctrl n = 3 (aged 10–12), O-Ctrl n = 5, O-Met n = 6.** (Methods text mentions 8 old
  controls enumerated; figure legends consistently use n=5 for O-Ctrl, n=5–6 for O-Met. One
  O-Ctrl monkey "succumbed to kidney failure on the 1,126th day.") The aging clocks were
  trained by "integrating data from the Y-Ctrl, M-Ctrl, and O-Ctrl cohorts (a total of 36
  monkeys)."
- **The treated group is SIX MONKEYS. All male.**
- Dose **20 mg/kg/day metformin via drinking water**, **1,200 days ≈ 3.3 years** on treatment
  within a 40-month study.
- Authors call it, verbatim, "a proof-of-concept study." Published as a *Resource* article.

**What was measured.** 68 biological parameters; MRI/CT; histology; pan-tissue transcriptomics
across 79 tissues; DNA methylomics; plasma proteomics; metabolomics; snRNA-seq;
Wisconsin General Test Apparatus cognitive tasks. Then bespoke "monkey aging clocks" were
built and the treated animals scored against them.

**Where the "6 years" comes from — read this carefully.**
- "This analysis estimated that metformin administration resets the **proteinAge** of treated
  monkeys by an average of **6.41 years**" — that is a *plasma proteomic clock*, not the brain.
- Frontal-lobe **DNAmAge** "restored by an average of **6.10** years"; then lung 5.11, kidney
  cortex 4.90, liver 3.95, skin 2.65.
- **transcriptAge** deltas by tissue: Achilles tendon 5.31, liver 4.14, bronchus 3.71, muscle
  3.56, lung 3.40 years.
- So "brain got 6 years younger" = a methylation-clock delta in the frontal lobe of six
  monkeys, on a clock the same group trained on 36 monkeys from the same colony. It is not a
  measured functional or clinical outcome.

**Authors' own limitations, verbatim — the most useful sentence in the paper:**
"We have noted that metformin potentially slows aging at molecular and tissue levels in males,
but **conclusive evidence of functional rejuvenation is sparse**, and a full understanding of
its side effects in humans remains elusive. **The study also omitted assessments of monkey
mortality** and the long-term effects following metformin cessation."
→ **Lifespan was not an endpoint. Mortality was not assessed.** A study that did not measure
survival was reported worldwide as evidence a drug slows aging.

**COI, verbatim:** "J.C.I.B., S.H., C.R.E., P.R., and A.H. are employees of **Altos Labs**.
S.H. is a founder of the non-profit **Epigenetic Clock Development Foundation, which has
licensed several of his patents from UC Regents and distributes the mammalian methylation
array.**"
→ The headline endpoint of this paper is an epigenetic clock number; a co-author holds
licensed patents on epigenetic clocks and distributes the array used to generate them. That is
a direct interest in the endpoint, not merely in the compound. Funding is otherwise Chinese
public science (NSFC, MOST, CAS) — no metformin seller involved.

**Cross-caliber flag.** Barzilai, describing this same paper on 2025-08-12: "Aging was delayed
by **eight years on the transcriptomic level**." The paper's transcriptAge deltas top out at
5.31 years (Achilles tendon); the ~6.4 figure is proteomic and the ~6.1 figure is frontal-lobe
methylation. There is no 8-year transcriptomic number in the paper. Treat "8 years" as a
misquotation in circulation.

**Other species.** Metformin lifespan results in C. elegans, Drosophila and inbred C57BL/6
mice are positive but strain- and dose-dependent; the ITP's stated reason for its own null is
the use of genetically heterogeneous mice at 3 sites with log-rank testing. No dog, no
naked mole rat, no other primate replication exists.

## 11. Gaps — searched for and not found

1. **No target trial emulation, 2024–2026, of the actual Bannister comparison** (metformin
   initiators vs matched non-diabetics, all-cause mortality). Every TTE I found uses an
   active diabetes comparator (sulfonylurea) or asks a different question (CKD continuation).
   The specific claim that launched the field has been tested by two conventional cohort
   studies and by zero modern TTEs.
2. **No RCT of metformin in healthy non-diabetic adults with a hard clinical outcome has
   ever reported.** VA-IMPACT is the first and reads out 2029 — and its population is
   prediabetes with established ASCVD, not healthy ageing.
3. **TAME has no ClinicalTrials.gov registration**, no NCT number, no protocol paper with a
   locked statistical analysis plan that I could locate. For a trial with an 11-year public
   profile and a named FDA-negotiated endpoint, the absence of a registry entry is itself
   the most informative fact available.
4. **No primary AFAR document stating TAME's budget or funds raised to date.** Every budget
   figure in circulation ($30–50M, $50–75M, $8M/yr) traces to journalism or interview, not to
   a grant document or audited statement.
5. **No published ITP re-analysis showing metformin benefit in the second half of life**,
   despite Barzilai citing one as existing in Aug 2025. Either unpublished, in press, or
   mischaracterised. Should be re-checked.
6. **No effect sizes reachable for Stevenson-Hoare 2023** at the 3/5/20-year timepoints
   (paywalled); the direction and the reversal point are from the abstract only.
7. **No female monkeys.** The only primate metformin study is male-only, n=6 treated, and its
   authors say so.
8. **No head-to-head human trial of metformin vs exercise**, or of metformin-plus-exercise vs
   exercise alone with a hard outcome — despite two RCTs showing metformin degrades the
   exercise response. Given that exercise has far better human outcome evidence than
   metformin, this is the most consequential missing trial on this line.
9. **No retraction or erratum on Bannister 2014.** It stands uncorrected and continues to be
   cited. The correction to the record exists only as separate papers by other groups.

## 12. Cross-caliber issues to hand to the writer

- **"Diabetics on metformin outlive non-diabetics"** is a 15% *median-survival-time ratio*
  from a log-logistic AFT model over a **2.4-year median follow-up truncated at 5.5 years**,
  with BMI unadjusted and progressors censored. It is repeated as though it were a lifespan
  observation. The crude rates it rests on differ by 0.8 deaths per 1000 person-years
  (14.4 vs 15.2).
- **The Denmark/UK discrepancy is one cell, not four.** 3 of Bannister's 4 crude mortality
  rates replicate almost exactly in Denmark. Only the metformin arm doesn't (14.4 vs 24.9).
  Anyone attributing the disagreement to "different populations" (as Barzilai does, citing
  UK vs Danish obesity rates) has to explain why the other three cells match.
- **"A 2025 target trial emulation confirms metformin longevity"** — the comparator is
  sulfonylurea, N=438, women only, E-value 1.88. It confirms metformin beats a drug the
  same literature says is harmful. It does not touch the non-diabetic comparison.
- **"Metformin made monkey brains 6 years younger"** — proteomic clock 6.41 y / frontal-lobe
  methylation clock 6.10 y, in **6 male monkeys**, on clocks the authors built, in a study
  whose authors state it "omitted assessments of monkey mortality."
- **Barzilai's "eight years on the transcriptomic level"** (Aug 2025) exceeds any transcriptAge
  number in the Cell paper (max 5.31 y, Achilles tendon).
- **Barzilai's "people on metformin are twice as likely to reach age 90"** (Aug 2025,
  pre-announcing the J Gerontol paper) vs what that paper reports: HR 0.70 (0.56–0.88) for
  death before 90 versus **sulfonylurea**, in 438 women. "Twice as likely" and "vs other
  people with diabetes" are both looser than the published estimate.
- **DPP/DPPOS diabetes prevention (−17%) vs DPP/DPPOS mortality (HR 0.99) and MACE (HR 1.03).**
  The drug demonstrably works on the disease and demonstrably moves nothing downstream over
  21 years. Any article on metformin-for-longevity that omits this is misleading by omission.
- **Konopka's VO2max attenuation was p = 0.08.** It is widely cited as a significant finding.
  The mitochondrial respiration and insulin-sensitivity results are the significant ones.
- **MASTERS Table 2 relative-strength figures were wrong in the original** and corrected by
  corrigendum. Use the corrected values.

## 14. Suggested framing for the writer

The metformin story is the cleanest available case study of **tier inflation** in longevity
science, and it is unusually well documented because the reversal is published, the architect
gives dated on-the-record interviews, and the decisive trial's non-existence is verifiable
from a public registry.

Three beats:
1. **The claim was born industry-funded, unadjusted for BMI, censored on progression, and
   truncated at 5.5 years** — and the authors said most of that themselves in the limitations
   section that nobody read.
2. **It failed replication twice, in ways that explain the original.** Denmark shows the
   anomaly is confined to Bannister's metformin arm. Wales shows the benefit exists inside
   3 years and reverses after 5 — precisely inside Bannister's window.
3. **The trial designed to settle it never started, and the randomised human evidence that
   does exist is null.** DPPOS: 21 years, HR 0.99 mortality, HR 1.03 MACE. METFORAGING 2026:
   null on the clock too. Meanwhile the drug measurably blunts the adaptations to the one
   intervention with unambiguous human outcome evidence.

The honest bottom line is not "metformin does nothing." It is: **after ~30 years of use by
hundreds of millions of people, the geroprotective claim rests on one unreplicated
observational study, a null mouse trial, and six male monkeys — and the people most confident
about it agree you shouldn't take it if you're under 50 or trying to get fitter.**
